## Objective
Chapters 9 and 10 predate the Ch4–8 councils and have drifted badly. Rewrite them so they become grounded, actionable descriptions of **how a Node is born** (Ch9) and **how The Collective comes into being** (Ch10). The rewrite must be consistent with the settled architecture, honest about the real-world hostility a new community will face, and anchored in **strategic nonviolence**.

## Register (user's direction)
- **Structured, with a softer voice.** Use real sections for phases and steps a reader can follow, written in Ch1's warmer voice, with more narrative latitude than Ch2–8.
- These chapters describe *suggested approaches* that are grounded in evidence. They are not guarantees and not a fixed script.
- No bold pseudo-headings. Tables are welcome. Cite only real, verifiable sources.

## User's decisions on nonviolence and protection
- **Strategic nonviolence**, in the line of Sharp and Chenoweth & Stephan: nonviolence chosen because it is more effective and more sustainable, and because it protects the legitimacy, safety and cohesion of a young community. This is not presented as a purely moral absolute.
- The chapters must address **instruments of protection and their nuances**, for example:
  - legal observers and documentation;
  - rapid legal response and defence funds;
  - unarmed civilian protection and community safety teams;
  - de-escalation training;
  - neighbour alliances and solidarity networks;
  - media and transparency;
  - evacuation and continuity plans; protecting children and vulnerable people;
  - digital and operational security;
  - physical safety (fire, structural).

  The nuances include:
  - protection drifting into militarisation;
  - who watches the protectors;
  - security culture vs. paranoia;
  - infiltration vs. false accusations;
  - the boundary between protection of persons and the community's nonviolent stance;
  - individuals' legal rights vs. the community's collective posture.

  - L3 mints Exergy Credits (Proof-of-Thermodynamic-Work, demurrage). L4 never mints.
  - L4 runs only ratified workflows within actuation envelopes, and follows the priority order: floors and baseline first, export last.

  - The Genesis Compact is accepted unanimously by the founders and recorded in L5. It holds an entrenched core: survival baseline, ecological floors, right to refuse work, right of exit, privacy, due process, non-adversarial treatment of non-stewards.

- **Ch7 (L6):**
  - Draft → read-back → human signs.
  - Oasis shares components with the real interface but has **distinct modes**: reality mode is unmistakable, the game never holds real keys, simulated data is tagged, and game mechanics never appear in reality mode.

  - A lawful cooperative-style entity, not a "Special Purpose Company".

  - Oasis is an honest invitation, not a disguise.
  - A workflow proven in simulation becomes a *proposal* that must be ratified.

## The Council (7)
1. **Peace & Conflict Studies Scholar (social violence and strategic nonviolence).**
   - Ground the chapters in how legacy aggression actually unfolds against communities that hold land and run their own systems:
     - direct violence: evictions, raids, armed neighbours, arson;
     - structural violence (Galtung): zoning, code enforcement, de-banking, criminalisation;
     - covert repression: infiltration, surveillance, disinformation (e.g., COINTELPRO).
   - Use documented cases, such as the MOVE bombing (Philadelphia, 1985), Christiania, the ZAD of Notre-Dame-des-Landes, Brazil's MST, and Standing Rock.
   - Anchor **strategic nonviolence** in the genesis process: a nonviolent discipline commitment in the Genesis Compact, training, escalation ladders, the evidence base, and the instruments of protection with their nuances.
   - Address violence *inside* young communities: founder coercion, charismatic capture, abuse.
2. **Intentional Communities Historian & Founding Practitioner.**
   - Why founding groups fail: founder dominance, money, land, interpersonal conflict, unwritten agreements, burnout. Use real literature only, e.g., Kanter's *Commitment and Community* and Christian's *Creating a Life Together*.
   - Realistic timelines and phase ordering; what "autonomy" honestly means; onboarding later members; what to write down at founding.
3. **Serious Games & Player Psychology Researcher (individual dimension).**
   - Whether game-learned skills transfer to the real world, and what the evidence says.
   - Consent and transparency toward players; compulsion and dark patterns; play-as-labour ethics.
   - Location data; how Character Creation feels to a real person.
   - Whether simulated trials can responsibly gate real-world permissions.
4. **Ethical & Cultural Guide (collective dimension).**
   - Founding ritual and the Genesis Compact as a shared story; the "escape" narrative vs. good faith toward neighbours.
   - Honest dealing with players; cultural plurality in what each new Node becomes.
   - Grief and loss when a genesis fails.
5. **Social Activist (solarpunk ethos), the contrasting voice.**
   - Grassroots, prefigurative and joyful, from the tradition of mutual aid, community gardens, repair cafés, community solar and direct action.
   - Challenge over-engineering, techno-solutionism, legalism and caution where they would stall real people.
   - Ask what can start *this weekend* with no servers; whether the chapters centre the already-marginalised; where aesthetics, celebration and hope belong.
   - Push back on other council members where warranted.
6. **Sovereign Stack Architect (adjacency and consistency).**
   - Enforce Ch1–8: genesis as a governed process ratified in L5, not a self-assembling organism.
   - Correct layer roles in every phase; Oasis modes; the Genesis Compact; Ch6 privacy; Ch8 entity and export rules.
   - Produce a drift table.
7. **Legal & Regulatory Counsel.**
   - Securities and labour-law hazards of converting game contributions into equity or governance weight.
   - Consumer protection for game buyers; privacy law for location and behavioural data (e.g., GDPR-type regimes).
   - Land acquisition, zoning, building codes and permits during genesis.
   - The legal side of protection: rights during evictions and raids, legal observers, documentation, and the limits on what a community may lawfully do to protect people and property.
   - Jurisdiction-agnostic, with examples.

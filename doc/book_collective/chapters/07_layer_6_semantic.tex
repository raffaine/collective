\chapter{Layer 6: The Semantic Layer}

Layer~6 is where people meet the Stack. It translates between human expression and the structured records of Layer~5; it hosts local and federated deliberation in the Agora; and it carries the community's ratified decisions outward to Layer~7, the layer that faces the legacy world.

Layer~6 interfaces strictly with Layer~5 below it and Layer~7 above it. Under the law of Strict Adjacency, it does not read the Thermodynamic Ledger, it does not communicate directly with the Orchestrator, and it possesses zero knowledge of physical hardware or network device addresses. Everything it knows about the physical world arrives as cryptographically signed structured records from Layer~5's governance daemon (\texttt{col-kmsd}), which in turn received them from Layer~4. In turn, all outward instructions intended for external corporate, legal, or municipal entities cross the perimeter boundary into Layer~7's legacy adversary daemon (\texttt{col-adversaryd}).

Layer~6 is executed by a dedicated system daemon, \texttt{col-commonsd} (The Semantic Commons Daemon). Running within the node's local application runtime, \texttt{col-commonsd} orchestrates local deliberation, plain-language intent compilation, deterministic read-back generation, and semantic translation across two strictly defined Unix domain sockets:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Inward Governance Socket}\newline
  \texttt{/run/collective/ipc/l5\_l6.sock}: Connects \texttt{col-commonsd} directly to \texttt{col-kmsd} within the internal secure enclave. Through this boundary, Layer~5 delivers task offers, deliberative proposals, capability listings, and federated treaty negotiation intents. In return, Layer~6 submits signed member ballots, liquid delegations, ratified Ricardian Treaty packages ($\mathrm{Treaty\_CID}$), and bilateral credit window pacts.
  \item \textbf{Outward Legacy Socket}\newline
  \texttt{/run/collective/ipc/l6\_l7.sock}: Connects \texttt{col-commonsd} outward across the internal DMZ firewall into VLAN~40, where \texttt{col-adversaryd} operates. Through this boundary, Layer~6 transmits instructions for legacy-facing actions (purchases, statutory filings, vendor contracts) and legal instruments wrapping ratified inter-node treaties (Bilateral Mutual-Aid Agreements, Subchapter~T Shared Services Agreements, and conservation easements). In return, Layer~7 forwards incoming legacy documents (invoices, legal notices, regulatory correspondence) into Layer~6's untrusted intake pipeline.
\end{itemize}

Layer~6 is also the only layer in which a machine---specifically, a local language model---stands directly between people and their own governance. A mistranslated proposal can become binding law, a biased summary can steer a debate, and an injected adversarial instruction can impersonate a decision. The entire architecture of the layer is therefore organized around a single foundational rule: \textit{the machine assists, people author, and only people sign}.

Earlier drafts described Layer~6 as the interface that would one day be ``the only interface humanity needs to interact with the world.'' That claim is decisively withdrawn. Layer~6 is the community's interface to its own infrastructure and governance. The soil, assemblies, physical craft, and unmediated human company remain the primary ways people meet the living world; Layer~6 serves them and must never replace them. As legacy systems recede, Layer~7 will shrink, but Layer~6 will remain one tool among many, not a totalizing digital environment.

\section{Design Principles}

The following principles govern all operations of Layer~6 and \texttt{col-commonsd}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textit{Human authorship.} Any record entering Layer~5 is authored and signed by a human being. The machine may draft; it never signs, and it never submits on anyone's behalf.
  \item \textit{Read-back before signing.} A person signs only after seeing a plain-language rendering generated deterministically from the exact structured payload they are about to sign.
  \item \textit{Grounded or silent.} Generated explanations must cite the Layer~5 records they rely on. A claim with no verifiable cryptographic source is never displayed.
  \item \textit{Deterministic where safety matters.} Safety-critical alerts use fixed, tested wording, never generated text.
  \item \textit{Graceful degradation.} Every function of governance, deliberation, and translation remains fully usable without a language model.
  \item \textit{Privacy by construction.} Layer~6 renders each person's own records to that person and public aggregates to everyone. It never assembles a graph of who trusts, delegates to, or votes for whom.
  \item \textit{Plurality.} Vocabularies, interfaces, and deliberative formats can be adapted to each Node's languages, traditions, and culture.
  \item \textit{The right to unmediated space.} Any conversation, council, or assembly can be held without machine processing, transcription, or digital capture.
  \item \textit{Mundane semantic camouflage at the legacy boundary.} All outward instructions dispatched across \texttt{/run/collective/ipc/l6\_l7.sock} must translate internal sovereign terminology into unremarkable, conventional cooperative legal expressions, protecting the Node from regulatory exposure and legal consolidation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What Layer 6 Does Not Do}

Layer~6 does not read telemetry or device identifiers, does not explain the Orchestrator by inspecting it directly, and does not tally votes or store identity. A person's cryptographic keys live exclusively on a hardware device that person controls, and cryptographic signing happens there. Layer~6 provides the rendering interface to that device but never holds the private keys. When Layer~6 explains why an irrigation workflow changed, it is rendering an alert and rationale that Layer~5 passed upward, not inspecting Layer~4 or querying physical sensors.

\subsection{The Semantic Model and Its Provenance}

To explain events in human terms, Layer~6 maintains a semantic model linking concepts such as ``Greenhouse B,'' ``winter food supply,'' and ``Pump 3,'' and the relationships between them, for example that Greenhouse B supplies a fifth of the community's winter vegetables. Every fact in this model arrives as a structured description from Layer~5. The model contains no raw telemetry, no physical MAC or IP addresses, and nothing Layer~5 did not explicitly provide.

The model is expressed in JSON-LD \parencite{w3c2020jsonld}, which lets each Node publish its own vocabulary while mapping it to a small shared core needed for interoperation. Heavyweight ontology reasoning is avoided on edge hardware. More importantly, a single comprehensive ontology is avoided on principle: whoever defines the categories shapes what can be said. Layer~6 therefore supports several overlapping graphs, so that a river can be described at the same time as an irrigation source in one vocabulary and as a sacred living entity in another, without either description overriding the other. Vocabularies are governed objects. They are adopted and amended through Layer~5, each with a recorded rationale.

\section{Translation}

\subsection{From People to Layer 5: Draft, Read-Back, Sign}

Every record a person sends to Layer~5 follows the same sequence:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The person expresses intent in any supported language or modality: typed text, speech, a paper form transcribed by a helper, or a structured interface form.
  \item A drafting model converts the intent into a structured payload of the type Layer~5 expects, such as a proposal, delegation, appeal, self-report, or inter-node treaty covenant. For proposals that change operational workflows, Stewards with the relevant skills may refine the resulting draft BPMN, as Chapter~6 describes; drawing BPMN is never required to take part.
  \item If the intent is ambiguous, the drafting model stops and asks for clarification. It does not guess. Unresolved ambiguity is referred to a human interpreter.
  \item The payload is rendered back to the person in plain language by a deterministic renderer: fixed templates driven by the payload's fields, not a language model. The read-back therefore shows what will actually be signed, not what the model believes it wrote.
  \item The person confirms, edits, or abandons the draft. Confirmation is an Ed25519 signature generated on the person's own hardware device.
  \item The signed payload is dispatched to Layer~5 with a verified provenance tag (Section~\ref{sec:l6-provenance}).
\end{enumerate}
The deterministic read-back is the most vital architectural safeguard in Layer~6. It defends against model error, semantic drift, and prompt injection alike, because nothing reaches Layer~5 that the signer did not verify rendered directly from the structured payload itself.

\subsection{Provenance of Text}
\label{sec:l6-provenance}

Every piece of text displayed in Layer~6 carries one of four provenance tags, rendered prominently in the user interface:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{human\_authored}: written directly by a person and signed with their private key;
  \item \texttt{machine\_drafted\_human\_approved}: drafted by a model, verified via deterministic read-back, and signed by a person;
  \item \texttt{machine\_generated}: produced autonomously by a language model and not approved by a person, such as background explanations and debate summaries;
  \item \texttt{system\_authenticated}: issued by Layer~5 and cryptographically signed, such as alerts, ratified decisions, and official voting tallies.
\end{itemize}
System-authenticated content is cryptographically signed by \texttt{col-kmsd}, preventing any participant or hostile actor from spoofing an official council notification.

\subsection{Languages, Dialects, and Oral Tradition}

Language models are strongest in the languages and registers most common in their training corpora and weakest in minority languages, regional dialects, and oral traditions. Layer~6 compensates in four ways. Each Node maintains a controlled vocabulary mapping its own colloquial idioms, dialect terms, and indigenous phrasing to the shared core schema. Communities may fine-tune their local models on community-approved corpora---such as recorded speech, local council records, and oral histories---only with the explicit consent of those whose words are used. Spoken contributions are transcribed and shown back for physical confirmation before they enter any governance record. Finally, human interpreters are treated as first-class participants in the Agora, not an afterthought.

\subsection{When Translation Fails or Is Disputed}

Any member may formally dispute a machine rendering, whether it is a debate summary that misstates a minority dissent or an explanation that misinterprets a council decision. A disputed rendering is immediately tagged across all interfaces, and the underlying structured Layer~5 record always prevails over any textual rendering. Translation disputes and systematic failures are fed directly into the model evaluation pipeline described in Section~\ref{sec:l6-models}.

\section{The Language Models}
\label{sec:l6-models}

\subsection{Running on Salvaged Hardware}

Layer~6 runs small, efficient language models compressed to fit within the memory bounds of salvaged and second-life edge hardware. Post-training quantization (such as 4-bit and 3-bit GPTQ and AWQ) reduces memory footprints severalfold while preserving core reasoning and summarization quality \parencite{frantar2023gptq}. A typical deployment allocates a compact model (3B to 7B parameters) on local community workstations for rapid drafting and speech transcription, and a slightly larger quantized model (13B to 30B parameters) on the settlement's primary server for grounded retrieval explanation. Inference latency is measured in seconds rather than milliseconds, and the user interface is explicitly structured to accommodate asynchronous deliberative rhythms rather than compulsive instant messaging.

\subsection{Inference as an Exergy Expenditure}

Every model inference consumes physical electricity. Layer~6 operates within a strict computational exergy allocation ratified in Layer~5 and budgeted by the Layer~4 Orchestrator like any other physical process (Chapter~5). When exergy availability contracts---during winter low-solar periods or grid emergencies---the inference budget automatically scales down. Layer~6 sheds computational load in a deterministic sequence: optional summaries and stylistic elaborations are suspended first; routine notice expansions follow. Drafting assistance, deterministic read-back templates, and safety-critical alert dispatch are never degraded, and the latter two operate without language models.

\subsection{Graceful Degradation}

If all language models become unavailable---due to total hardware exhaustion or extreme power rationing---every governance function in Layer~6 remains operational. Structured forms replace natural-language drafting, fixed templates replace explanatory generation, raw cryptographic records remain directly inspectable, and human interpreters, scribes, and physical paper ballots remain standing. A community that loses its computational models loses administrative convenience, never its democratic sovereignty.

\subsection{Model Governance and Supply Chain}

Model weights, system prompts, retrieval indices, and semantic vocabularies are governed communal assets. Adopting or modifying any model component requires a formal Layer~5 ratification accompanied by a rationale record. Every approved model artifact is indexed by its SHA-256 cryptographic hash, and \texttt{col-commonsd} refuses to load any weight file or prompt configuration whose hash does not match an active ratification record.

Before adoption, candidate models undergo transparent red-team evaluations. The evaluation measures translation accuracy across the settlement's specific languages, audits potential demographic biases, and tests robustness against adversarial prompt injection. Models trained on public internet datasets inevitably absorb corporate, colonial, and commercial biases \parencite{bender2021parrots,noble2018}; air-gapping a neural network does not cleanse it of its training assumptions. A local corpus provenance registry logs the exact origin and fine-tuning lineage of every deployed model, and all generated outputs display the identifier of the specific model that produced them.

\section{Security}

\subsection{The Air-Gap Claim, Corrected}

Although Layer~6 language models run locally without connections to proprietary cloud APIs, Layer~6 is by no means insulated from adversarial attack. It ingests unstructured text from human participants and, through Layer~7, from the external legacy world: commercial contracts, vendor invoices, regulatory citations, and legal correspondence. Unstructured text ingested by a language model can harbor malicious instructions designed to hijack the model's execution context---a vulnerability known as indirect prompt injection \parencite{greshake2023injection}, recognized as a primary threat to autonomous systems \parencite{owasp2023llm}.

\subsection{Separation of Reading and Drafting}

To neutralize indirect prompt injection, Layer~6 enforces strict physical and architectural isolation between models that ingest untrusted text and models that draft governance payloads.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The Intake Model:} Operates within an isolated, unprivileged sandbox. It processes incoming legacy documents delivered across \texttt{/run/collective/ipc/l6\_l7.sock} as well as external public petitions. Its output is constrained by strict grammar engines to emit only fixed schema fields (e.g., currency figures, dates, entity names, cited statutory provisions). It possesses zero drafting capabilities and has no access to Layer~5 proposal templates.
  \item \textbf{The Drafting Model:} Receives only verified human input and typed schema fields. It is architecturally barred from reading raw, untrusted text arriving from Layer~7.
\end{itemize}
Neither model holds cryptographic private keys, and neither can submit a transaction to Layer~5. All governance actions require physical user confirmation through deterministic read-back, which serves as the final barrier: even if an adversary successfully manipulated an intermediate schema extraction, the resulting aberration would be rendered explicitly in plain language on the signer's personal hardware device before signature execution.

\subsection{Private Data and Retrieval}

Data categorized as private under Layer~5 governance rules is permanently excluded from retrieval indices, vector embeddings, and model context windows. This prohibition covers secret assembly ballots, liquid delegation edges, personal care-leave records, capacity self-reports, task refusals, and pairwise cryptographic pseudonyms. Semantic search is strictly scoped to the authenticated user and to the specific council in which they are actively participating. Deliberation logs are end-to-end encrypted, subject to automated retention policies ratified in Layer~5, and never incorporated into model training sets without unanimous consent.

\subsection{Red-Teaming in Oasis}

Security defenses are subjected to continuous empirical evaluation. Adversarial test suites---including sophisticated prompt injections, semantic evasion payloads, and counterfeit legacy notices---are procedurally simulated in Oasis and executed against candidate models before deployment, ensuring that \texttt{col-commonsd} maintains robust boundary hygiene.

\section{The Agora: Local Councils and Bioregional Assemblies}

The Agora encompasses the deliberative spaces of Layer~6, coordinated by the \texttt{col-commonsd} daemon. The structure of each deliberative forum directly reflects the decision rules and trust parameters established in Layer~5 (Chapter~6).

\subsection{Deliberative Formats}

Layer~6 provides a rich typology of deliberative tools: open forums for idea generation, structured argument maps that place competing claims and counter-evidence side by side, and sortition-selected Citizens' Assemblies drawn by lot to deliberate on complex communal questions on behalf of the broader settlement \parencite{fishkin1991}. It hosts Future Generations panels charged with ecological stewardship and Review Councils adjudicating restorative justice petitions. For physical assemblies, Layer~6 provides real-time transcription, multi-lingual captioning, and verifiable record archiving.

\subsection{The Machine's Role in Deliberation}

Within the Agora, language models serve as neutral administrative facilitators: transcribing speech, translating languages, retrieving verified evidence from the Layer~5 archive, and generating balanced summaries. The machine's participation is bound by four strict constraints:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Summaries must follow a structured schema, isolating consensus points, majority arguments, and minority dissents into separate fields with direct source citations;
  \item Any summary destined for an official rationale record must receive formal approval from representatives of each represented perspective; dissenters explicitly approve the rendering of their dissent;
  \item The model is forbidden from ranking arguments, recommending specific votes, or synthesizing persuasive rhetoric on its own initiative;
  \item All model-assisted text is permanently tagged as \texttt{machine\_generated}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Unmediated Spaces and the Protocol of Refusal}

Any participant can inspect the raw transcript underlying any generated summary. Furthermore, any council, working group, or assembly may designate a meeting or channel as \textit{unmediated}. In an unmediated space, \texttt{col-commonsd} disables all recording, transcription, summarization, and vector indexing; only the minutes manually recorded and approved by the human participants enter the permanent record. Councils may also place sensitive topics under the Protocol of Refusal (Chapter~6), extending cryptographic and social protection to sacred cultural knowledge, sensitive ecological habitats, and private communal traditions.

\subsection{Rendering Governance Without Exposing People}

Layer~6 displays to each member their personal standing, Reputational Wealth balance, active delegations, and private ballot receipts. To the collective, it displays only authorized public aggregates: ratified proposals, recorded rationale statements, voter participation percentages, and anonymized ballot tallies. It refuses to construct or display global reputation leaderboards, and it never publishes the social graph of who trusts, delegates to, or votes with whom.

\subsection{The Bioregional Agora Across Trust Rings}

Deliberation in the Sovereign Stack does not terminate at the perimeter of the local settlement. Through the Federated Meshing architecture established in Chapter~6, Layer~6 expands the Agora outward across the 7 concentric Trust Rings ($R_0$ through $R_6$):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Ring $R_0$ (Personal and Household Sanctuary):} Private domestic agreements, caregiver circles, and pairwise mutual support pacts.
  \item \textbf{Ring $R_1$ (The Local Node / Hearth Assembly):} The primary sovereign assembly governing local land, thermodynamic floors, and internal chore allocation.
  \item \textbf{Ring $R_2$ (Valley and Municipal Neighborhood Mesh):} Adjacent sister settlements sharing micro-watersheds, local transit corridors, and tool libraries.
  \item \textbf{Ring $R_3$ (Watershed Congresses):} Hydrological sub-basins coordinating seasonal water flows, riparian forest restoration, and flood prevention across dozens of Nodes.
  \item \textbf{Ring $R_4$ (Bioregional Guilds and Assemblies):} Macro-regional coordination across mountain ranges and river basins (up to $150$~km), deliberating heavy industrial casting, mobile diagnostic healthcare, and high-voltage electrical balancing.
  \item \textbf{Ring $R_5$ (Continental Federation):} Inter-bioregional treaties governing continental rail corridors, long-distance mineral exchanges, and shared technological repositories.
  \item \textbf{Ring $R_6$ (Planetary Commons):} Global ecological compacts, open-source scientific standards, and planetary climate stabilization protocols.
\end{itemize}

Deliberating across outer Trust Rings ($R_3 \dots R_5$) requires managing high latency and intermittent packet transport over directional LoRa and Reticulum mesh links. The Bioregional Agora operates asynchronously: debates unfold over weeks rather than hours, structured argument maps accumulate cross-valley testimonies, and sortition panels drawn by lot from uninvolved peer Nodes adjudicate regional resource trade-offs.

Most crucially, the Bioregional Agora is where sister Nodes negotiate and ratify the terms of horizontal economic federation: establishing bilateral credit ceilings $[-C_{u \to v}, +C_{v \to u}]$ on Layer~3 Bilateral Clearing Channels, defining shared resource taxonomies $\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{res}}$, and deliberating periodic Commons Jubilees that rebalance structural trade asymmetries across the bioregion without monetary extraction.

\section{Ricardian Inter-Node Treaties}

Horizontal federation between sovereign Nodes cannot rest upon informal verbal understandings, nor can it submit to centralized global ledgers. Instead, inter-node economic and logistical pacts are authored, compiled, and executed as \textit{Ricardian Inter-Node Treaties} \parencite{grigg2004}.

\subsection{The Tripartite Contract Architecture}

A Ricardian Inter-Node Treaty binds three distinct representations into a unified, cryptographically immutable artifact:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Treaty\_CID} = \mathrm{SHA256}\left( \mathrm{Text}_{\mathrm{prose}} \parallel \mathrm{Schema}_{\mathrm{JSON-LD}} \parallel \mathrm{Invariants}_{\mathrm{BPMN}} \right)
\label{eq:ch7_treaty_cid}
\end{equation}
The three facets of the treaty address three distinct audiences:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Human Prose ($\mathrm{Text}_{\mathrm{prose}}$):} The natural-language covenant articulating bioregional friendship, mutual-aid commitments, shared ecological boundaries, and dispute-resolution protocols. Authored and debated by human Stewards in the Agora, this text defines the moral and social intent of the alliance.
  \item \textbf{Machine Schema ($\mathrm{Schema}_{\mathrm{JSON-LD}}$):} A strongly typed, machine-verifiable data structure defining concrete operational parameters: peer identities ($AN_{\mathrm{id}}$), bilateral mutual credit windows $[-C_{u \to v}, +C_{v \to u}]$, acceptable resource categories ($\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{res}}$), delivery verification predicates, and thermodynamic clearing ratios denominated in $3.6\,\mathrm{MJ}$ ($1.0\,\mathrm{kWh}$) Exergy Credit units.
  \item \textbf{Process Invariants ($\mathrm{Invariants}_{\mathrm{BPMN}}$):} A formal state machine expressed in BPMN 2.0 XML and compiled into WebAssembly bytecode. Executed by Layer~4's deterministic Wasmtime runtime, these invariants govern delivery time-outs, physical actuation locks, and autonomous curtailment triggers.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The 4-Stage Compilation Pipeline}

The authoring and deployment of an inter-node treaty follows a strict 4-stage compilation pipeline executed by \texttt{col-commonsd}:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Stage 1: Intent Authoring and Agora Deliberation ($\mathrm{Text}_{\mathrm{prose}}$):} 
  Stewards representing peering Nodes initiate discussions in the Bioregional Agora (Ring $R_3$ or $R_4$). The drafting model helps structure clauses and identify potential boundary ambiguities. Once consensus is reached, the prose agreement is displayed via deterministic read-back and confirmed with threshold Ed25519 cryptographic signatures from each settlement's ratified delegates.
  
  \item \textbf{Stage 2: Deterministic Schema Extraction ($\mathrm{Schema}_{\mathrm{JSON-LD}}$):}
  \texttt{col-commonsd} extracts the precise operational parameters from the ratified agreement: peer network addresses, credit ceiling bounds $[-C_{u \to v}, +C_{v \to u}]$, permitted resource types $\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{res}}$, and physical sensor verification criteria. This extraction is validated against rigid JSON-LD schema definitions; any ungrounded parameter or syntactic ambiguity halts the pipeline for human resolution.
  
  \item \textbf{Stage 3: Process Invariant Compilation ($\mathrm{Invariants}_{\mathrm{BPMN}}$):}
  The operational invariants (e.g., maximum delivery latency $\tau_{\mathrm{deadline}}$, two-phase sensor receipt verification, and escalation paths to sortition review panels) are compiled into BPMN 2.0 execution graphs. These graphs are transformed into deterministic WebAssembly binaries designed for Layer~4 runtime enforcement.
  
  \item \textbf{Stage 4: Canonical Hashing and Dispatch:}
  The three artifacts are canonicalized using RFC~8785 JSON Canonicalization Rules and concatenated into a byte stream. The SHA-256 hash of this stream yields the canonical Content Identifier: $\mathrm{Treaty\_CID}$. The completed treaty package is simultaneously dispatched across two internal socket boundaries:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Inward to Layer~5}\newline
    \texttt{/run/collective/ipc/l5\_l6.sock}: \texttt{col-kmsd} ingests the treaty, registers the peer's capability advertisements into the Bioregional Path-Vector Protocol (BPVP) routing table, and instructs Layer~3 (\texttt{col-storaged}) to initialize a pairwise Bilateral Clearing Channel ($BCC$).
    \item \textbf{Outward to Layer~7}\newline
    \texttt{/run/collective/ipc/l6\_l7.sock}: \texttt{col-commonsd} generates an operational directive instructing \texttt{col-adversaryd} to wrap the treaty in an appropriate legacy legal chassis, as detailed below.
  \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\section[Directing Layer 7: Legal Carapace]{Directing Layer 7: Legal Carapace and Mundane Semantic Camouflage}

The Sovereign Stack rejects ideological purism. Individual Nodes do not operate in a legal vacuum; they inhabit real jurisdictions subject to municipal zoning codes, corporate filing mandates, utility regulations, and tax scrutiny. When a community decides to engage with the external world---or when sister Nodes establish physical trading interties across legacy terrain---Layer~6 directs Layer~7 to erect a protective legal carapace.

\subsection{Instructing the Adversary Daemon Across the DMZ}

All communication between Layer~6 and Layer~7 flows across the boundary Unix domain socket:
\begin{center}

\section{Oasis: A Shared Interface, Distinct Modes}

The interface used by players to simulate and govern virtual bioregions in Oasis is constructed from the identical software components and design patterns as the interface Stewards utilize in the real physical Node. Skills acquired within the simulation transfer directly to real-world democratic governance, while edge cases and interface failures encountered in Oasis expose flaws in deliberative protocols before they can cause physical harm.

However, sharing architectural components does not mean conflating operational realities. Layer~6 enforces strict modal segregation:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Visual and Audible Demarcation:} Reality mode operates within an unmistakable, persistent visual frame and unique color temperature that the Oasis simulation engine is hardcoded never to render;
  \item \textbf{Physical Key Signing:} All actions affecting the physical settlement require explicit signing on the user's personal hardware device; Oasis holds zero cryptographic signing keys for physical infrastructure;
  \item \textbf{Data Tagging:} All state variables generated within the game simulation are immutably tagged as simulated data (Chapter~3);
  \item \textbf{Purge of Gamification:} Game mechanics---such as experience points, leaderboards, and leveling milestones---are strictly excised from reality mode.
\end{itemize}

User interface improvements developed and evaluated within the Oasis simulation are introduced to the physical Node only through formal proposal, deliberation, and ratification in Layer~5. In this manner, each sovereign settlement retains the autonomy to adapt its semantic metaphors, deliberative idioms, and aesthetic forms to its unique cultural traditions, while the underlying mathematical and cryptographic protocols remain seamlessly federated across the living bioregion.

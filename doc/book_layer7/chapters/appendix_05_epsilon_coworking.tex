\chapter{Appendix Epsilon: The Coworking Scenario}

\section{The Legacy Dependency (Technical \\\& Cultural)}
The Epsilon Node relies on legacy commercial real estate markets. Dependencies include fiat-denominated long-term leases, commercial property insurance mandated by state law, municipal zoning classifications for office space, and traditional facility management vendors. Culturally, there is an expectation of centralized, hierarchically managed workspaces and traditional employment norms.

\section{The Parasitic Bridge (Technical \\\& Legal)}
Layer 7 creates a temporary interface via an LLC wrapper acting as a tenant. The LLC signs the fiat lease and holds the required commercial insurance. Behind this legal shield, the space is managed by a Layer 4 DAO, which pools member contributions in crypto, automatically liquidating the exact amount of fiat required for monthly lease and utility payments through a Layer 7 Fiat-Crypto Bridge. Traditional facility managers are paid via API-triggered fiat transfers based on cryptographic proof-of-work.

\section{The Decoupling Trigger}
Decoupling occurs when the local Sovereign Stack acquires full legal ownership of the property (via buyout or cooperative transition) or when the node's internal mesh economy generates sufficient capital to migrate to a fully sovereign, self-owned campus. The exact metric is the Node Capitalization Ratio exceeding 110\% of the property's market buyout cost.

\section{Orchestration \\\& Ethical Mechanics}
The orchestration uses cryptographic gateways to enforce access based on DAO membership rather than traditional keycards. Ethically, the transition must not displace existing small businesses; the node actively integrates local entrepreneurs into its cooperative model, offering subsidized access to Sovereign Stack resources to ease the memetic shock of transitioning from extractive rent to shared stewardship.

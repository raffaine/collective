\chapter{Open-Source Franchise Export: ``The Speedee Service System Without the Rent''}
\label{ch:l7-open-source-franchising}

\epigraph{The true measure of an invention's maturity is not the height of the patent wall erected around it, but how effortlessly another community can replicate it in an afternoon and improve it by nightfall.}{---\textcite{love1986mcdonalds} and \textcite{illich1973}, \textit{Operational Ergonomics vs.\ Convivial Commons}}

How does a post-capitalist civilization scale across a watershed or a continent? Under Layer~7 capitalism, operational replication takes the form of the \textbf{corporate franchise}: in 1948, Richard and Maurice McDonald invented the \textit{Speedee Service System}---a genuine triumph of industrial ergonomics that choreographed griddle thermal mass, cold-chain staging, prep station geometry, and sanitation loops \parencite{love1986mcdonalds}. Yet when Ray Kroc financialized that operational science into a corporate franchise empire, the knowledge was locked inside proprietary manuals, coupled to real-estate rent extraction, and enforced through rigid corporate uniformity that stripped local kitchens of culinary creativity.

In Dialogue~7 (\texttt{D7}) of \textit{The Architecture of Boundless Time} and Cross-Cutting Invariant~\textbf{CCI-5}, the Horizon Council established \textbf{Open-Source Franchising (``The Speedee Service System Without the Rent'')}. By separating the \textbf{cybernetic execution engines} of Layers~4 and~5 (\texttt{col-execd} and \texttt{col-kmsd}) from the \textbf{societal compiler/decompiler} of Layer~6 (\texttt{col-commonsd}) and protecting the exported knowledge bundles with Layer~7's \textbf{Reciprocal Copyleft Carapace}, any emerging neighborhood can pull a battle-tested node template from the global commons, adapt it to its local scavenged hardware in minutes, and contribute empirical thermodynamic improvements back to the federation at zero licensing rent.

\section{Layer 7 Anti-Enclosure Defense: Reciprocal Copyleft Licensing}
\label{sec:l7-anti-enclosure-copyleft}

Why don't legacy corporations simply scrape the Three Sovereign Libraries, patent a minor tweak, and sell closed derivatives back to the public? Layer~7 wraps every exported artifact in a legally enforceable \textbf{Tri-License Anti-Enclosure Shield}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item All software, WASM modules, BPMN state machines, and Layer~5 policy templates are licensed under the \textbf{GNU Affero General Public License v3 (\texttt{AGPL-3.0})} \parencite{agplv3}, forcing any network-facing derivative to publish its complete source;
    \item All Stage~II Right-to-Repair PCB designs, CAD assemblies, and Stage~III machine specifications are licensed under the \textbf{CERN Open Hardware Licence v2 --- Strongly Reciprocal (\texttt{CERN-OHL-S-2.0})} \parencite{cern_ohl_v2}; and
    \item All content-addressed publication timestamps are notarized into public cryptographic witness logs, establishing prior art that invalidates any subsequent third-party patent filing worldwide.
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Appendix 01: Scenario Alpha - Distributed Fabrication}

\section{The Legacy Dependency (Technical \\\& Cultural)}
Alpha Fabrication requires physical inputs that the Sovereign Stack cannot immediately produce: raw materials (e.g., PLA filament, aluminum stock, CNC tooling) and the legacy logistics networks (postal services, freight) required to deliver them. Culturally, there is a reliance on traditional intellectual property (IP) frameworks and fiat-based supply chain contracts.

\section{The Parasitic Bridge (Technical \\\& Legal)}
Layer 7 executes API Parasitism to scrape legacy supplier databases (e.g., industrial metal suppliers, Web2 logistics APIs) to identify the cheapest and most reliable material sources. Legal Wrappers are used to sign delivery contracts, while the Fiat-Crypto Bridge handles the automated, just-in-time payment of legacy invoices using funds escrowed by the decentralized fabrication DAO. This shields the sovereign fabrication nodes from fiat banking de-platforming.

\section{The Decoupling Trigger}
The Decoupling Trigger for raw material supply chains is the \textit{Circular Material Autonomy Metric}. Complete decoupling occurs when local sovereign recycling infrastructure (e.g., plastic shredders and filament extruders) and locally sourced biomaterials can supply 95\% of the fabrication network's input requirements, eliminating the need for legacy freight and fiat supplier contracts.

\section{Orchestration \\\& Ethical Mechanics}
Orchestration is handled by Layer 4/5 logic executing smart contracts that trigger Layer 7 APIs to order materials only when internal stockpiles fall below critical thresholds. Ethically, the system must navigate the extraction of materials from legacy supply chains without contributing to exploitative labor practices in the old world; thus, cryptographic oracles are tasked with verifying the ethical sourcing of legacy inputs before triggering the parasitic bridge.

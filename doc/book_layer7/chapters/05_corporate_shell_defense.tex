\subsection{How the Dual-Chassis CLT + Cooperative Carapace Shields Enveloping Node Hypervisors}

In Dialogue~6 (\texttt{D6}), the Council established that physical space is governed internally by a \textbf{domain-blind Enveloping Layer~5 Node} that leases bounded acoustic (\texttt{cB}), electrical (\texttt{mW}), thermal (\texttt{mK}), and spatial (\texttt{mm}) capabilities to co-located child nodes (\texttt{Scenario Zeta}, \texttt{Scenario Epsilon}, \texttt{Scenario Xi}). In Phase~3, the \textbf{Dual-Chassis Model} mirrors this exact cybernetic separation in legacy property law:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The Landholding Chassis (External Counterpart of the Enveloping Node Hypervisor):} A Community Land Trust (CLT) or Perpetual Purpose Trust holds sole, undivided fee-simple title to the real estate (\texttt{SCN-ZETA-LODGING}). Its charter contains an absolute restraint against speculative alienation. Just as the internal Enveloping Node Hypervisor is domain-blind to child-node craft recipes, the CLT does not engage in commercial baking or machining; it governs only the physical land and building envelope.
    \item \textbf{The Operational Chassis (External Counterpart of Polycentric Child Nodes):} Independent Worker or Multi-Stakeholder Cooperatives (or $N=1$ artisan associations) execute 99-year Ground Leases or room-bay sub-leases with the CLT. Each ground lease incorporates the Enveloping Node's physical boundary invariants (\texttt{EnvelopingBoundaryLeaseV50}: e.g., $4{,}200\,\mathrm{cB}$ quiet hours, $9{,}600{,}000\,\mathrm{mW}$ breaker cap) and a mandatory Federal Arbitration Act (9~U.S.C.~\S\S~1--16) / Brazilian Lei~9.307/1996 dispute resolution clause binding all boundary conflicts to Layer~5's \textbf{Two-Speed Jurisprudence} (\textit{Fast-Path} L2 hazard preemption and \textit{Slow-Path} Semantic Diff precedent compilation).
\end{enumerate}

Because no individual steward holds a divisible fee-simple fractional share, judicial partition lawsuits are legally impossible. Furthermore, because the operational cooperative's bylaws vest statutory voting membership strictly in active inducted stewards ($N_s$) and prohibit non-patron equity shares, external investors can never buy voting control over the node.

\section{Labor Bifurcation and the \textit{Alamo} FLSA Defense}
\label{sec:l7-alamo-labor-bifurcation}

In \textit{Tony and Susan Alamo Foundation v. Secretary of Labor}, 471 U.S. 290 (1985) \parencite{alamo1985}, the U.S. Supreme Court held that individuals staffing commercial businesses on behalf of a foundation in exchange for food, lodging, and care were statutory employees under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), notwithstanding their sincere testimony that they were voluntary community members. Parallel labor doctrines apply under the Brazilian \textit{Consolida{\c{c}}{\~a}o das Leis do Trabalho} (CLT) unless protected by genuine cooperative structure under \texttt{Lei 12.690/2012} \parencite{brazil_lei12690}.

Layer~7 eliminates misclassification liability through strict \textbf{Labor Bifurcation}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Internal Commons Stewardship (Membrane~2 Only):} Stewards tending community gardens, cooking in the communal hearth for residents, caring for children or elders, writing open-source Scenario Zero code, or retrofitting internal tools perform domestic mutual aid and civic self-provisioning. They perform zero labor for Layer~7's commercial export accounts.
    \item \textbf{Residual Commercial Export Labor (Membrane~1 Interface):} Stewards who voluntarily claim tasks producing residual goods or services sold to external Layer~7 customers are registered as bona fide worker-members of the cooperative (receiving statutory W-2 wages / Subchapter~T patronage allocations in the US, or protected worker-cooperative draws with mandatory social-security floors under \texttt{Lei 12.690/2012} in Brazil), with full workers' compensation and payroll tax compliance.
\end{enumerate}

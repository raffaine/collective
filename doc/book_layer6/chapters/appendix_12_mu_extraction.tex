\chapter{Appendix Mu: Resource Extraction \\\& Harvesting}

\section{The Legacy Paradigm \\\& Its Failures}
Legacy extraction UIs were disjointed dashboards operated by remote corporations, disconnected from the ecological reality of the extraction site. These systems prioritized throughput and profit metrics, obscuring the thermodynamic and environmental costs. Operators suffered from cognitive overload due to complex, centralized SCADA systems that required constant cloud connectivity.

\section{The Incremental Automation Pathway}
\textbf{Phase 1: Localized Sensor Meshes.} Transitioning to offline-first sensor networks that provide real-time, local data to on-site operators without relying on corporate cloud infrastructure.
\textbf{Phase 2: Algorithmic Ecological Triage.} The interface actively filters non-critical data, highlighting only ecological anomalies and equipment degradation, reducing operator fatigue.
\textbf{Phase 3: Autonomous Ephemeral Harvesting.} Zero-click, ambient coordination between automated extractors and human supervisors, with the interface only surfacing when thermodynamic limits or ecological boundaries are approached.

\section{The Human Boundary (Limits of Automation)}
The decision to initiate extraction in sensitive zones cannot be automated. The interface forces a "Hard Ethical Stop," requiring multi-party cryptographic signatures from local stakeholders before extraction protocols can be engaged. The UI purposefully presents the projected thermodynamic and ecological costs in raw, un-abstracted terms before allowing the human to proceed.

\section{Interface Mechanics (The "How")}
The UI relies on heavily degraded, ultra-low-power text modes (thermodynamic rendering) since extraction often occurs in remote, energy-scarce microgrids. State is maintained via CRDTs across the local mesh.
This reveals a missing mechanism: **Emergency Override UIs under Extreme Hardware Degradation**. How does the interface function when the physical screen is damaged or power is critically low during a physical crisis?

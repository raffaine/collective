\chapter{Appendix 15: Scenario Omicron - The Kinship Protocol}

\section{The Legacy Paradigm \& Its Failures}
In the legacy system, trust is outsourced to centralized data brokers (Equifax, Experian) and state authorities (KYC). More insidiously, the legacy system deliberately merges human survival with economic and social coercion. Landlords, employers, and even spouses can weaponize their economic power to regulate how vulnerable people survive. If someone leaves an abusive partner or a toxic workplace, they risk homelessness and starvation. Conversely, early "Web3" reputation systems risk recreating a panopticon where high-status individuals weaponize their reputation. A sovereign Node requires a mathematically rigorous method of establishing Trust while cryptographically firewalling that Trust from basic human survival.

\section{The Incremental Automation Pathway}
Transitioning from manual trust to autonomous enforcement requires phased evolution. Phase 1 begins with manual human vouching and social consensus for resource access. Phase 2 introduces AI-assisted tracking of vouches and automated calculation of trust graph distance, warning users of high-risk interactions. Phase 3 implements fully autonomous cryptographic enforcement where physical "Key-Signing Parties" generate localized Web of Trust graphs, and the orchestrator automatically regulates access to high-risk assets based on cryptographic proximity.

\section{The Human Boundary (Limits of Automation)}
The AI must mathematically firewall reputation from survival. Layer 5 strictly prohibits gating survival resources (calories, emergency shelter) behind the Trust Ring. The orchestrator cannot deny a human being basic biological necessities based on their trust distance or slashed reputation. The human boundary mandates that algorithmic slashing only applies to high-risk, high-exergy assets (e.g., industrial tools, micro-lodges), ensuring that the system protects the commons without coercing the individual.

\section{Orchestration Mechanics (The "How")}
Scenario Omicron establishes "Trust Rings" using directed, weighted graphs. Decentralized Identifiers (DIDs) are attached to the human social graph via physical "vouching" (e.g., NFC handshakes during a shared meal). The C++ Oasis engine maintains an Adjacency Matrix and utilizes Breadth-First Search (BFS) distance calculations to gate access. If a user commits a griefing event, the engine recursively traverses the graph to execute "Social Slashing," penalizing the offender and their vouchers. To protect intimacy, a "Safe Harbor" severance protocol allows users to execute a \texttt{SeveranceIntent}, dissolving a graph edge without triggering a slashing penalty. This deep dive into graph traversal reveals that core Chapter 2 must explicitly define the "Safe Harbor" and "Social Slashing" primitives.

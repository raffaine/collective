\subsection{\texttt{ATECC608A} Enclave Isolation and Direct $\mathrm{I^2C}$ Verification}

On every Node SBC and workshop terminal, \texttt{col-kmsd} anchors root signing keys inside a
dedicated hardware cryptographic co-processor---canonically the \textbf{Microchip \texttt{ATECC608A}}
connected over \texttt{/dev/i2c-1} via PKCS\#11 \parencite{microchip_atecc608a} (or an
equivalent OpenTitan / discrete TPM~2.0 module on salvaged \texttt{x86\_64} hosts), paired with
personal NFC smartcards carried by stewards:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Internal TRNG Key Generation and Non-Exportability:} Private scalars are generated
        inside the secure element's internal hardware True Random Number Generator (TRNG) and
        permanently locked into Fuse/EEPROM slots with read-out disabled. Even if an attacker
        gains root access to the Linux kernel on the host SBC, the raw 256-bit private key never
        enters host DRAM; \texttt{col-kmsd} submits a 32-byte digest over $\mathrm{I^2C}$ and
        receives back only the 64-byte signature.
\item \textbf{Defeating User-Space Shared-Memory Spoofing:} As demonstrated in
        \texttt{verification/test\_remediation\_resolution.py}, trusting a user-space
        \texttt{/dev/shm} byte flag (\texttt{quality\_flags == 0x02}) alone allows a local
        process with shared-memory write access to spoof hardware provenance. In v3.1,
        \texttt{col-kmsd} verifies cryptographic signatures directly against the pinned public
        key roster burned into its local \texttt{ATECC608A} slot before honoring any hardware
        attestation or governance resolution.
\item \textbf{Physical Chassis-Tamper Zeroization:} Node~0 and clinical/guild anchor enclosures
        wire normally-closed physical micro-switches to GPIO tamper interrupts, zeroizing
        volatile SQLCipher vault keys within microseconds of unauthorized enclosure intrusion.
\end{itemize}

\section{Three-Tier DID Taxonomy and Pairwise \texttt{did:peer} Unlinkability}
\label{sec:ch2-did-taxonomy}

Early decentralized-identity systems made a fatal error: assigning each human being a single,
globally resolvable public identifier (often anchored on a public blockchain) and attaching
every credential, vote, and social connection to that single handle. Such a design gifts hostile
states, employers, and data brokers a turnkey social graph. In constitutional terms, it also
violates the associational privacy doctrine recognized in \emph{NAACP v.\ Alabama}
\parencite{naacp1958}: the safest membership roster against an abusive subpoena is a roster that
\textbf{structurally does not exist}.

\texttt{col-kmsd} implements W3C Decentralized Identifiers \parencite{w3c2022did} across three
strictly segregated scopes:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Infrastructure Node Identifiers (\texttt{did:mesh:...}):} Assigned strictly to
        \textit{physical routing nodes and communal stations} (never to human beings) to anchor
        \texttt{BPVP} capability advertisements and Layer~3 node-to-node Bilateral Clearing
        Channels.
\item \textbf{Ephemeral Session Identifiers (\texttt{did:key:...}):} Short-lived transport keys
        rotated per Companion VLAN~30 TLS/WebSocket session or offline guest-door HMAC lease.
\item \textbf{Pairwise Relational Identifiers (\texttt{did:peer:...}):} For every distinct
        bilateral peer relationship, guild council, care pod, or kinship covenant $c$, a
        steward's enclave deterministically derives an independent, uncorrelatable keypair
        $(sk_{u,c}, pk_{u,c})$ via keyed HMAC-based Extract-and-Expand Key Derivation (HKDF):
        \[
            sk_{u,c} = \mathrm{HKDF\text{-}Expand}\!\big(mk_u,\; \texttt{"COL5-DID-PEER-V31"} \parallel \mathrm{context\_salt}_c\big) \bmod \ell,
        \]
        sharing \textit{zero} public group elements across distinct contexts $c_1 \neq c_2$.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{definition}[Structural Data Non-Existence Across Governance Contexts]
\label{def:ch2-structural-nonexistence}
A governance Layer~5 vault satisfies Structural Data Non-Existence if no table, index, or log
maps a citizen's legal Layer~7 name to any \texttt{did:peer}, nor links a citizen's Water
Council \texttt{did:peer} $d_{u,\mathrm{water}}$ to their Perinatal Pod \texttt{did:peer}
$d_{u,\mathrm{delta}}$ or Healthcare \texttt{did:peer} $d_{u,\mathrm{chi}}$. Cross-domain
safety proofs execute solely via zero-knowledge \texttt{BBS+} selective disclosure
(Chapter~\ref{ch:l5-trust-rings}).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Cross-Council Pairwise \texttt{did:peer} Unlinkability]
\label{thm:ch2-pairwise-unlinkability}
Let $mk_u \in \{0,1\}^{256}$ be a steward's hardware-locked master seed, and let $c_1 \neq c_2$
be two governance councils with public transcripts $\mathcal{T}_{c_1}, \mathcal{T}_{c_2}$. Under
the pseudorandomness of $\mathrm{HKDF\text{-}SHA256}$ and the Decisional Diffie--Hellman /
discrete-logarithm hardness of the underlying curve, any polynomial-time adversary $\mathcal{A}$
observing $(\mathcal{T}_{c_1}, \mathcal{T}_{c_2})$ without $mk_u$ has negligible advantage
$\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{link}}_{\mathcal{A}}(\lambda) \le \epsilon_{\mathrm{HKDF}}(\lambda)$ in
determining whether $pk_{u,c_1}$ and $pk_{v,c_2}$ belong to the same human steward ($u = v$) or
two distinct stewards ($u \neq v$).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Because $mk_u$ is never exported from the secure element and $\mathrm{context\_salt}_{c_1} \neq
\mathrm{context\_salt}_{c_2}$ are domain-separated strings, replacing
$\mathrm{HKDF\text{-}Expand}(mk_u, \cdot)$ with a truly random function $R$ changes $\mathcal{A}$'s
output probability by at most $\epsilon_{\mathrm{HKDF}}(\lambda)$. Under $R$, the scalars
$sk_{u,c_1}$ and $sk_{u,c_2}$ are independent uniform elements in $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$, identically
distributed to $(sk_{u,c_1}, sk_{v,c_2})$ for independent $mk_u, mk_v$. Because signatures in
$\mathcal{T}_{c_1}$ and $\mathcal{T}_{c_2}$ are zero-knowledge of $sk$ beyond $pk = [sk]G$, the
joint distributions of public keys and transcripts are computationally indistinguishable.
\end{proof}

\section{\texttt{FROST} Threshold Ceremonies: Faraday Genesis and Vouching}
\label{sec:ch2-frost-ceremonies}

No single human---no founder, sysadmin, or guild master---may ever hold unilateral root power

eliminates single-key bottleneck risk via \textbf{Flexible Round-Optimized Schnorr Threshold
Signatures (\texttt{FROST})} \parencite{komlo2020frost} over Ristretto255/Ed25519.

Unlike naive multi-signature concatenation (whose byte size grows linearly with signer count $t$

public key $PK_{\mathrm{group}}$ is a single 32-byte curve point, and any $t$ participants
produce a single, standard 64-byte Schnorr signature $(R, z)$ in two communication rounds
\textit{without ever reconstructing the group secret scalar $s$ in any device's memory}.

\section{In-Person $(M, N)$ Shamir Social Key Recovery}
\label{sec:ch2-shamir-recovery}

Physical smartcards can be crushed in a sawmill, dropped into a river, or lost during an
evacuation (\texttt{Scenario Upsilon}). In legacy platforms, losing a hardware token forces
fallback to centralized ``customer support'' identity checks; in naive crypto wallets, losing a
seed phrase means permanent civic death.

Layer~5 resolves key loss without central authorities or biometrics via \textbf{$(M, N)$ Shamir Secret
Sharing} \parencite{shamir1979} (typically $3\text{-of-}5$) over a prime field $\mathbb{F}_p$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item At onboarding, the steward's enclave splits a recovery key-wrapping secret $K_{\mathrm{rec}}$
        into $N = 5$ polynomial evaluations $\{(x_i, f(x_i))\}_{i=1}^5$ ($\deg f = M - 1 = 2$)
        and seals each shard inside the hardware enclave of a chosen kinship or neighborhood
        guardian. Any $M - 1 = 2$ guardians colluding learn \textbf{zero Shannon bits} of
        $K_{\mathrm{rec}}$.
\item If the steward's card is destroyed, $M = 3$ guardians tap their smartcards physically in
        person with the steward at a local terminal, reconstructing a signed key-rotation intent
        that binds a fresh smartcard public key $pk_{\mathrm{new}}$ to the steward's encrypted
        standing backup.
\item \textbf{72-Hour Coercion Timelock Alarm:} Unless co-signed under an active Watershed
        Disaster Refuge state (\texttt{Scenario Upsilon}), key rotation enters a mandatory
        $259{,}200\,\mathrm{s}$ ($72\text{-hour}$) cooling-off window during which the old key
        (if still possessed by a live steward under social-engineering attack) can unilaterally
        veto the rotation with a single tap. During those $72\,\mathrm{hours}$, the steward's
        $B_{\mathrm{baseline}}$ meals, shelter, and care remain $10{,}000\,\mathrm{bp}$ active.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{invariant}[Zero-Bit Collusion Leakage and Coercion Veto Window]
\label{inv:ch2-shamir-safety}
Under $(M, N)$ Shamir recovery over $\mathbb{F}_p$: (i)~for any subset $S$ of guardians with
$|S| < M$, the mutual information $I(K_{\mathrm{rec}};\, \{f(x_i)\}_{i \in S}) = 0$ identically;
and (ii)~any recovery rotation lacking an active physical disaster attestation remains vetoable
by the primary hardware enclave for $\Delta_{\mathrm{cooloff}} = 259{,}200\,\mathrm{s}$ prior to
Sparse Merkle Tree key replacement.
\end{invariant}

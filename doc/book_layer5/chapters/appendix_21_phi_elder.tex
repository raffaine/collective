\chapter{Appendix 21: Scenario Phi - The Elder Commons}

\section{The Legacy Paradigm \& Its Failures}
In legacy systems, elders are treated as economic burdens and isolated in for-profit institutions, draining generational wealth and leading to extreme psychological neglect. This dynamic deprives younger generations of critical wisdom and context, while the nuclear family model causes catastrophic burnout for isolated caregivers.

\section{The Incremental Automation Pathway}
Phase 1 consists of fragmented, isolated familial caregiving. Phase 2 involves orchestrated scheduling applications to coordinate volunteers. Phase 3 implements fractional caregiving via the BPMN engine, which fractures heavy care burdens across the entire Trust Ring and algorithmically mints Value Tokens for the elder's passive negentropy (wisdom sharing and monitoring).

\section{The Human Boundary (Limits of Automation)}
The orchestrator can handle scheduling and ambient privacy-preserving monitoring, but the actual care---physical mobility assistance, cooking, storytelling, and human touch---must remain intimately human. Dignity, privacy, and consent are absolute limits that the AI cannot override.

\section{Orchestration Mechanics (The ``How'')}
Layer 5 policies mandate strict cryptographic vouching and credentials before allowing a caregiver to accept an \texttt{ElderSupportIntent}. Ambient mmWave radar sensors provide telemetry without cameras, strictly preserving privacy while enabling instantaneous fall detection. The BPMN engine fractures a 40-hour care requirement into 2-hour micro-tasks routed to multiple trusted DIDs. The Ledger values passive labor, minting tokens for elders who perform zero-kinetic wisdom offerings.

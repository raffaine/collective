\chapter{Concentric Trust Rings, Sybil-Resistant EigenTrust, and Blinded Capabilities}
\label{ch:l5-trust-rings}

\epigraph{Access control lists ask a feudal question: `Whose name stands on the master's roster?' Capabilities and relational webs of trust ask a convivial question: `What verified competence and reciprocal trust authorize this specific act, without exposing the actor to surveillance?'}{---Adapted from \textcite{miller2006robust} and \textcite{illich1973}}

Standard enterprise software governs permissions through Role-Based Access Control (RBAC): a
central administrator assigns boolean flags (\texttt{is\_admin}, \texttt{can\_operate\_cnc}) to
user rows in a database \parencite{saltzer1975protection}. In a decentralized commons, boolean
RBAC fails instantly. First, whoever holds the ``super-admin'' bit to edit the role table becomes
a de facto ruler. Second, binary membership breaks down across geographic distance: a neighbor
known face-to-face for three years in the workshop warrants different mutual-credit headroom than
an unverified node fifty kilometers upriver, yet both deserve dignified cooperation. Third,
open peer-to-peer networks face \textcite{douceur2002sybil}'s classic \textbf{Sybil Attack}, wherein a
single adversary fabricates thousands of synthetic identities that enthusiastically vouch for one
another.

Layer~5 (\texttt{col-kmsd}) abolishes boolean RBAC (\texttt{rbac\_prohibited: true} in
\texttt{core/5\_governance/trust\_gates.json}) and replaces speculative ``Soulbound Tokens''
with three rigorous mathematical structures: (1)~\textbf{Seven Concentric Trust Rings
($R_0\text{--}R_6$)} seeded by physical NFC ceremonies; (2)~\textbf{Hardware-Anchored Fixed-Point
EigenTrust} \parencite{kamvar2003} paired with \textbf{Ford--Fulkerson Max-Flow Cut Bounds}
\parencite{ford1956maximal}, proven empirically against bipartite and rank-sink Sybil attacks in
\texttt{verification/test\_eigentrust\_sybil.py}; and (3)~\textbf{BLS12-381 \texttt{BBS+}
Selective-Disclosure Verifiable Credentials} \parencite{camenisch2004signature,w3c2022vc}
compiled down into \textbf{24-byte PRF-blinded \texttt{ClaimHandle}} tokens over
\texttt{l4\_l5.sock}.

\section{Fixed-Point Graph Attenuation and Empirical Sybil Immunity}
\label{sec:ch3-eigentrust-sybil}

Within local settlement rings ($R_1\text{--}R_3$), \texttt{col-kmsd} evaluates weighted trust
paths using \textcite{dijkstra1959note}'s shortest-path algorithm in pure integer basis points
(\texttt{bp}, where $10{,}000\,\mathrm{bp} = 1.0000$). Along a path of directed vouch edges
$e_1, \dots, e_k$ ($k \le 3$) with edge trust weights $w(e_j) \in [0, 10{,}000]\,\mathrm{bp}$,
multiplicative path trust attenuates strictly via integer floor division:
\[
W_{\mathrm{path}}(e_1 \cdots e_k) = \left\lfloor \frac{W_{\mathrm{path}}(e_1 \cdots e_{k-1}) \cdot w(e_k)}{10{,}000} \right\rfloor_{\mathrm{bp}},
\]
requiring $N \ge 3$ vertex-disjoint vouch paths before granting high-trust ring elevation.

\subsection{Three Empirical Discoveries from \texttt{test\_eigentrust\_sybil.py}}

When scaling trust evaluation across federated meshes ($1{,}024+$ nodes), adversarial red-teaming
in \texttt{verification/test\_eigentrust\_sybil.py} uncovered three critical mathematical traps
that shaped the ratified v3.1 engine:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Bipartite $K_{25,25}$ Bypass of Triangle Clustering:} Naive Web-of-Trust systems
        attempt to detect Sybil botnets by flagging clusters with abnormally high triangle
        clustering coefficients. Our verification suite proved that a 50-bot adversary arranged
        as a complete bipartite graph $K_{25,25}$ achieves maximal internal cross-vouching while
        maintaining a triangle clustering coefficient identically equal to \textbf{$0\,\mathrm{bp}$
        ($0.0000$)}---because bipartite graphs contain zero odd cycles!
\item \textbf{Rank-Sink Absorption Under Unanchored Power Iteration:} Running unanchored Markov
        iteration $\mathbf{t}^{(k+1)} = C^T \mathbf{t}^{(k)}$ allows a 5-node cyclic Sybil
        ``rank sink'' (accepting inbound vouches while emitting zero outbound edges to honest
        nodes) to absorb \textbf{$100\%$ ($10{,}000\,\mathrm{bp}$)} of global network trust.
\item \textbf{Iteration Cutoff Under-Convergence:} On realistic clustered $1{,}024$-node
        community graphs, stopping EigenTrust at a hardcoded 15 iterations leaves large $L_1$
        residuals; exact convergence requires \textbf{$28\text{--}45$ residual-monitored iterations}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Anchored Fixed-Point EigenTrust and Max-Flow Cut Bounds}

To close all three vectors simultaneously, \texttt{col-kmsd} computes \textbf{Pre-Trusted Anchored
EigenTrust} in 64-bit fixed-point basis points anchored to hardware-seeded $R_0/R_1$ genesis
roots $\mathbf{p}$ ($\sum_i p_i = 10{,}000\,\mathrm{bp}$) with teleportation damping
$\alpha = 1{,}500\,\mathrm{bp}$ ($15\%$):
\begin{equation}
t_j^{(k+1)} = \left\lfloor \frac{(10{,}000 - \alpha) \sum_{i} C_{ij}\, t_i^{(k)} + \alpha \cdot 10{,}000 \cdot p_j}{10{,}000^2} \right\rfloor_{\mathrm{bp}},
\label{eq:ch3-anchored-eigentrust}
\end{equation}
iterating dynamically until $\|\mathbf{t}^{(k+1)} - \mathbf{t}^{(k)}\|_1 \le 1\,\mathrm{bp}$
(retaining \textbf{$92.6\%$ ($9{,}260\,\mathrm{bp}$)} of total trust on honest stewards even
under active cyclic sink attack), and wraps the result in a \textbf{Ford--Fulkerson Max-Flow Vouch
Capacity Cap} \parencite{ford1956maximal}.

\begin{theorem}[Sybil Trust Amplification Bound Under Anchored Cut-Capacity Clamping]
\label{thm:ch3-sybil-bound}
Let $V = V_H \sqcup V_S$ partition the trust graph into honest stewards $V_H$ (containing all
pre-trusted roots $\mathrm{supp}(\mathbf{p}) \subseteq V_H$) and an arbitrary Sybil subgraph
$V_S$ of size $|V_S| = M$ (including bipartite $K_{m,m}$ or cyclic rank sinks). Suppose at most
$g$ compromised boundary edges $\partial(V_H \to V_S)$ cross from $V_H$ into $V_S$, each capped
at vouch flow capacity $c_{\mathrm{edge}}$ basis points. Under anchored iteration
\eqref{eq:ch3-anchored-eigentrust} with $\alpha \in (0, 10{,}000)$ followed by max-flow clamping
from source $\mathbf{p}$, total aggregate trust assignable to $V_S$ satisfies:
\[
\sum_{s \in V_S} T_{\mathrm{final}}(s) \;\le\; \min\!\left(
    \frac{10{,}000 - \alpha}{\alpha} \sum_{(u,s) \in \partial(V_H \to V_S)} C_{us}\, t_u^*,\;\;
    g \cdot c_{\mathrm{edge}}
\right),
\]
which is strictly independent of the number of synthetic identities $M = |V_S|$ minted inside $V_S$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Summing the stationary anchored equation $\mathbf{t}^* = (1 - \tilde{\alpha}) C^T \mathbf{t}^* +
\tilde{\alpha} \mathbf{p}$ (where $\tilde{\alpha} = \alpha / 10{,}000$) over $s \in V_S$ and
noting $p_s = 0$ for all $s \in V_S$ yields
$\sum_{s \in V_S} t_s^* = (1 - \tilde{\alpha}) \sum_{u \in V_H} \sum_{s \in V_S} C_{us} t_u^* +
(1 - \tilde{\alpha}) \sum_{r \in V_S} (\sum_{s \in V_S} C_{rs}) t_r^*$. Since row stochasticity
gives $\sum_{s \in V_S} C_{rs} \le 1$, rearranging yields $\tilde{\alpha} \sum_{s \in V_S} t_s^*
\le (1 - \tilde{\alpha}) \sum_{(u,s) \in \partial(V_H \to V_S)} C_{us} t_u^*$. Second, by the
Max-Flow Min-Cut Theorem \parencite{ford1956maximal}, the cut $(V_H, V_S)$ separates source
$\mathrm{supp}(\mathbf{p})$ from every $s \in V_S$ with total cut capacity at most
$g \cdot c_{\mathrm{edge}}$. Clamping trust flow by minimum cut capacity therefore bounds total
Sybil weight by $\min(\cdot, g \cdot c_{\mathrm{edge}})$ regardless of internal edges or $M$.
\end{proof}

\section{\texttt{BBS+} Zero-Knowledge Credentials, SMT Revocation, and BKT}
\label{sec:ch3-bbs-plus-bkt}

How does a steward prove they are certified to isolate a $380\,\mathrm{V}$ HVDC bus, operate a
biochar pyrolysis kiln, or receive a peanut-free tiffin meal without publishing ``Soulbound
Tokens'' on a global ledger or revealing their identity?

\texttt{col-kmsd} issues W3C Verifiable Credentials signed with \textbf{multi-message \texttt{BBS+}
signatures over pairing-friendly BLS12-381 curves} (\texttt{bbs-2023-zkp})
\parencite{camenisch2004signature,goldwasser1989zkp,w3c2022vc}:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Selective Disclosure and Randomized Unlinkability:} A single credential signs a
        vector of attributes $(m_1, \dots, m_L)$ (holder secret commitment, skill URI, tier,
        expiry, non-revocation leaf index). At presentation time, the steward generates a
        randomized zero-knowledge proof-of-knowledge revealing \textit{only} the required safety
        predicate (e.g., $\mathtt{skill} = \mathtt{HVDC\_HotWork\_VC}$) while blinding the
        signature itself so two presentations cannot be linked.
\item \textbf{256-Bit Sparse Merkle Tree (SMT) Non-Revocation:} When a credential expires or is
        paused due to prolonged skill-fade, \texttt{col-kmsd} updates its 256-bit SMT root
        without broadcasting who changed; non-membership in the revoked set is proven in zero
        knowledge in $\le 15.0\,\mathrm{ms}$ p99 on an ARM64 Cortex-A72.
\item \textbf{Scenario Kappa Integer BKT and 3-of-3 Anti-Nepotism Issuance:} Minting a hazardous
        \texttt{L1}/\texttt{L2} Skill VC requires three objective gates: (1)~integer \textbf{Bayesian
        Knowledge Tracing} mastery $P(L_t) \ge 9{,}500\,\mathrm{bp}$ ($95.00\%$)
        \parencite{corbett1995bkt}; (2)~an unannounced physical E-Stop fault-isolation reflex
        $\le 1{,}500\,\mathrm{ms}$; and (3)~a \textbf{3-of-3 hardware signature} from one \texttt{L3}
        Master plus two independent stewards from distinct households.
\end{itemize}

\begin{lstlisting}[language=C++,caption={Pure-integer Bayesian Knowledge Tracing (BKT) mastery update in \texttt{col-kmsd} (zero floats).},label={lst:ch3-integer-bkt}]
#include <cstdint>

namespace collective::l5 {

struct alignas(8) IntegerBktParamsBp {
    uint16_t p_init_bp{1500U};    // Prior mastery P(L_0) = 15.00% (2B)
    uint16_t p_transit_bp{1800U}; // Learning transition P(T) = 18.00% (2B)
    uint16_t p_slip_bp{600U};     // Mastery slip P(S) = 6.00% (2B)
    uint16_t p_guess_bp{1200U};   // Novice guess P(G) = 12.00% (2B)
};
static_assert(sizeof(IntegerBktParamsBp) == 8);

[[nodiscard]] constexpr uint16_t StepIntegerBktMasteryBp(
    uint16_t prior_bp, const IntegerBktParamsBp& p, bool observed_pass) noexcept
{
    constexpr uint64_t kOne = 10000ULL;
    const uint64_t L = (prior_bp > 10000U) ? 10000ULL : prior_bp;
    uint64_t num = 0ULL;
    uint64_t den = 1ULL;

    if (observed_pass) {
        // P(L|pass) = L*(1 - S) / [L*(1 - S) + (1 - L)*G]
        num = L * (kOne - p.p_slip_bp);
        den = num + (kOne - L) * p.p_guess_bp;
    } else {
        // P(L|fail) = L*S / [L*S + (1 - L)*(1 - G)]
        num = L * p.p_slip_bp;
        den = num + (kOne - L) * (kOne - p.p_guess_bp);
    }
    const uint64_t post_obs_bp = (den > 0ULL) ? ((num * kOne) / den) : L;
    // Posterior after learning transition: P(L_t) = P(L|obs) + (1 - P(L|obs))*P(T)
    const uint64_t next_bp = post_obs_bp + ((kOne - post_obs_bp) * p.p_transit_bp) / kOne;
    return static_cast<uint16_t>(next_bp > kOne ? kOne : next_bp);
}

} // namespace collective::l5
\end{lstlisting}

\section{The 24-Byte Blinded \texttt{ClaimHandle} Across \texttt{l4\_l5.sock}}
\label{sec:ch3-claim-handle}

Once \texttt{col-kmsd} verifies a steward's \texttt{BBS+} zero-knowledge presentation, trust-ring
distance, and absence of \texttt{SafeHarbor} proximity conflicts, how does it authorize
\texttt{col-execd} (Layer~4) to advance the corresponding BPMN \texttt{UserTask} while keeping
Layer~4 $100\%$ blind to citizen identities?

Across opcode \texttt{0x0503} (\texttt{L5\_GRANT\_CLAIM\_HANDLE}) on \texttt{l4\_l5.sock},
\texttt{col-kmsd} transmits a cache-aligned 64-byte \textbf{\texttt{L5ToL4ClaimCommand}}
embedding the ratified \textbf{24-byte \texttt{ClaimHandle}} (Figure~\ref{fig:ch3-blinded-claim} and
Listing~\ref{lst:ch3-claim-command}), reconciling the exact wire contract specified in the
Layer~4 companion volume:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{\texttt{ephemeral\_token\_id} (8~bytes):} Derived inside \texttt{col-kmsd}'s
        \texttt{ATECC608A} enclave via
        $\mathrm{PRF}_{K_{\mathrm{L5}}}(\mathrm{pairwise\_did} \parallel \mathrm{task\_id} \parallel \mathrm{nonce})$.
        Layer~4 and Layer~3 observe a uniform random 64-bit integer with zero linkability across
        tasks.
\item \textbf{\texttt{capability\_mask} (8~bytes):} Encodes up to 64 verified domain safety
        clearances as a bitmask, checked by \texttt{col-execd} in a single bitwise \texttt{AND}
        instruction \parencite{miller2006robust,iec61508}.
\item \textbf{Distance-Adaptive \texttt{expiry\_hlc} (8~bytes):} As proven in Round-3 verification
        (\texttt{round3/test\_d2\_autonomy\_deadlock.py}), a naive fixed 60-tick claim lease
        suffers $>65\%$ premature expiration on large rural campuses when a steward walks across
        fields with a wheelbarrow. \texttt{col-kmsd} therefore computes
        $\mathtt{expiry\_hlc} = t_{\mathrm{now}} + \Delta_{\mathrm{base}} + \Delta_{\mathrm{haul}}(d_{\mathrm{parcel}})$.
        If a steward changes their mind and invokes \texttt{UNCLAIM\_GUILT\_FREE} (or simply lets
        \texttt{expiry\_hlc} elapse), the token returns silently to \texttt{UNASSIGNED} with zero
        demerit record.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{invariant}[Total DID Non-Leakage Across the Downward Orchestrator Socket]
\label{inv:ch3-did-nonleakage}
No frame emitted on \texttt{l4\_l5.sock} (\texttt{0x0501..0x0506}) ever contains a string or
hash deterministically computable from a citizen's \texttt{did:peer} without the hardware-locked
enclave key $K_{\mathrm{L5}}$; additionally, any expired or \texttt{UNCLAIM\_GUILT\_FREE}
\texttt{ClaimHandle} triggers zero standing deduction in \texttt{col-kmsd}.
\end{invariant}

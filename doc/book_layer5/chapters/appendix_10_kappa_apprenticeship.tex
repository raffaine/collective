\chapter{Appendix 10: Scenario Kappa - The Apprenticeship Protocol}

\section{The Legacy Paradigm \& Its Failures}
The legacy educational system relies on centralized institutional gatekeepers issuing static, easily forged paper degrees. This credentialism traps students in decades of fiat debt and is disconnected from local thermodynamic reality, failing to produce citizens with the physical survival skills required by their immediate communities.

\section{The Incremental Automation Pathway}
\textbf{Phase 1: Manual Trust.} Stewards personally observe and vouch for apprentices, keeping manual ledgers of hours worked.

\textbf{Phase 2: AI-Assisted Policy.} A BPMN orchestrator tracks hours via physical check-ins and prompts Stewards when a capstone review is due.

\textbf{Phase 3: Autonomous Cryptographic Enforcement.} Layer 5 automatically issues a W3C Verifiable Credential upon multi-signature capstone approval, instantly unlocking physical access to restricted machinery without administrative intervention.

\section{The Human Boundary (Limits of Automation)}
While the BPMN engine can track time and telemetry (BLE presence), the actual assessment of tacit knowledge---such as the safe operation of an induction furnace---cannot be automated. The final capstone review must remain a human multi-signature process, bound to the physical reality of the craft.

\section{Orchestration Mechanics (The "How")}
The protocol uses a localized Web of Trust (Chapter 2) and Soulbound Tokens (Chapter 3) to mint W3C Verifiable Credentials. Once the apprentice accumulates sufficient L2 telemetry (hours logged), a multi-signature transaction is triggered requiring an $n$-of-$m$ consensus from certified Masters. Upon signature aggregation, the credential is bound to the apprentice's DID (Chapter 1), updating the L5 policy state to cryptographically grant them L2 actuator access to specialized tools.

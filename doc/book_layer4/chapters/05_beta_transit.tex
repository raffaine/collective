\chapter{Scenario Beta: Decentralized Transit \& Logistics}

\section{The Legacy Paradigm \& Its Failures}
In the legacy system, human mobility is highly atomized and engineered for maximal extraction. Commuters move 4,000 pounds of steel to transport a single 170-pound human across sprawling infrastructure, resulting in immense exergy waste in the form of traffic congestion and carbon drag. These multi-ton personal vehicles remain parked and idle for over 95\% of their operational lifespan. When attempts are made to pool resources or share vehicles via legacy Web2 platforms (such as Uber, Lyft, or Turo), the corporate Layer 7 intermediaries extract 30\% to 50\% of the transaction value as rent. Safety is entirely outsourced to centralized, opaque background checks rather than relying on localized community trust, while liability rests heavily on the atomized individual rather than a resilient collective network.

\section{The Incremental Automation Pathway}

\subsection{Phase 1: Human-in-the-Loop (Manual Orchestration)}
At the onset, decentralized transit relies on manual vector matching. Users manually post their intended travel routes or vehicle availability on a shared Layer 3 ledger. The coordination of a rideshare or a peer-to-peer vehicle checkout demands direct communication and human-approved BPMN gateways. Escrow locks for distance traveled and vehicle depreciation are manually calculated, and physical keys are exchanged in person, preventing scalable efficiency.

\subsection{Phase 2: The Centaur (AI-Assisted)}
As the Node matures, localized routing algorithms begin parsing \texttt{TransitIntents}. The AI calculates optimal detour routes and potential spatial-temporal overlaps among users. It suggests matches that minimize thermodynamic waste and drafts the requisite smart contracts for vehicle borrowing. However, the AI cannot autonomously execute the match; it strictly requires human cryptographic signing to accept the route deviation and lock the exact escrow parameters before proceeding.

\subsection{Phase 3: Full Autonomy}
In full autonomy, the Orchestrator algorithmically matches temporal-spatial vectors without human intervention. The system seamlessly resolves scheduling conflicts, enforces trust-based routing dynamically, and executes keyless handoffs. By provisioning cryptographically signed BLE digital keys directly to a borrower's mobile device, the vehicle is unlocked, and its ignition is actuated autonomously upon validation of the ledger escrow and safety credentials.

\section{The Human Boundary (Limits of Automation)}
The strict limits of AI jurisdiction in transit are profound. The AI can calculate the thermodynamic efficiency of a detour, but it cannot override the physical safety or agency of the human driver. The Orchestrator is cryptographically forbidden from matching riders who do not share a minimum threshold of mutual nodes in the Web of Trust (e.g., 1st or 2nd-degree connections). Furthermore, the AI cannot physically verify the state of a returned vehicle beyond L2 telemetry (e.g., OBD2 data); human Stewards must physically attest to the cleanliness and safety of the cabin, providing a strict hard-stop to purely algorithmic quality control.

\section{Orchestration Mechanics (The "How")}
Scenario Beta's execution relies heavily on spatial-temporal vector math integrated tightly into the BPMN orchestrator. \texttt{TransitIntents} are processed using advanced graph traversal algorithms on the local Web of Trust, calculating the shortest cryptographic trust path to ensure passenger safety. Detour viability is strictly evaluated using a thermodynamic threshold: a match is mathematically rejected if the shared route distance fails to constitute a high percentage of the total detour distance.
Upon successful matching, the system manages vehicle borrowing through asynchronous state machines. The orchestrator tracks Layer 2 MQTT telemetry (GPS coordinates, odometer deltas, state-of-charge) and triggers Layer 3 CRDT wallet transfers precisely when the physical trajectory completes. Zero platform fees are extracted; the escrow directly routes exergy-backed tokens to the Steward, deducting only a strict fractional cut bound for the Layer 7 Social Purpose Corporation's insurance pool.

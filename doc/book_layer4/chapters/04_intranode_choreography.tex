\chapter{High-Density Intra-Node Choreography: Hundreds of Workflows on One Node}
\label{ch:intranode-choreography}

\epigraph{``Variety can destroy variety; only requisite variety in the regulator can absorb the proliferating disturbances of the world without collapsing the organism into central paralysis.''}{\textsc{W. Ross Ashby}, \textit{An Introduction to Cybernetics} (1956)}

A persistent architectural fallacy in early workflow engines and domestic automation prototypes is the
assumption of low concurrency. When designers sketch a pedagogical example---irrigating a raised pea bed
or simmering a single kettle of potage---it is tempting to imagine that a sovereign node executes three or
four sequential checklists at human walking speed. Under that illusion, linear array scans,
single-instance workflow locks, and imperative \texttt{if/else} dependency ladders appear harmless. Yet
the moment a household, neighborhood commons, or cooperative campus internalizes real material
reproduction, a single governed node does not run six workflows: it runs \emph{dozens to thousands} of
intricate, thermodynamically coupled BPMN 2.0 lifecycles simultaneously
\parencite{omg2011bpmn,vanderaalst1998application}.

Consider what occurs concurrently beneath the roof and soil of one living site. Four staggered legume and
root-crop beds occupy distinct photo-thermal phenology cohorts; a rotating thermophilic compost drum and a
Black Soldier Fly Larvae (\texttt{BSFL}) bioconversion ramp digest kitchen peelings; a $24\,\mathrm{h}$
acid-assisted collagen broth simmers beside a batch of lacto-fermenting cabbage crocks whose acidity must
cross below $4{,}600\,\mathrm{mpH}$; a solar Maximum Power Point Tracking (\texttt{MPPT}) charge
controller balances a $\mathrm{LiFePO_4}$ battery bank against cistern pump head and acoustic quiet hours;
woodshop joinery commissions queue behind moisture-content verification gates; apprentice skill rubrics
await peer co-signatures; and background software-forge builds verify deterministic WebAssembly binaries.
Every one of these processes possesses long-lived state, conditional boundary timers, physical station
leases, and material Bill-of-Materials (\texttt{BOM}) dependencies.

If these hundreds of concurrent lifecycles were allowed to mutate one another's memory directly or invoke
point-to-point callbacks across workflows, an $N$-workflow node would degenerate into an
$\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ web of race conditions, priority inversions, and unreplayable side effects
\parencite{hewitt1973universal,miller2006robust}. Conversely, if a central imperative controller attempted
to micromanage every cross-domain interaction, it would violate Viable System Model (\texttt{VSM}) System
1/System 2 subsidiarity \parencite{beer1972,beer1979} and overwhelm human stewards with notification
fatigue \parencite{weiser1996calm}. This chapter establishes the mathematical and systems architecture by
which \texttt{col-execd} sustains hundreds to thousands of concurrent workflow instances on a single
low-power single-board computer (\texttt{SBC}) within a deterministic $10\,\mathrm{Hz}$
($100\,\mathrm{ms}$) tick budget, achieving rich ecological coupling with zero point-to-point spaghetti.

\section{Quantitative Anatomy of Single-Node Workflow Density}
\label{sec:workflow-density-anatomy}

To engineer \texttt{col-execd} against realistic physical load rather than synthetic micro-benchmarks, the
Architectural Council audited every recurring and on-demand process across all twenty-five Sovereign
Scenarios from initial household adoption through bioregional campus maturity. Workflow concurrency on a
single governed node scales across four institutional stages, partitioned into six functional domains: (1)
Metabolic and Ecological, (2) Kitchen and Bioprocess, (3) Energy, Water, and \texttt{HVAC}, (4)
Fabrication and Logistics, (5) Care, Somatic, and Apprenticeship, and (6) Governance and Software Forge.

\subsection{Scaling Laws from Stage 1A to Stage 3}

In \textbf{Stage 1A (Household Seed, 1 Lot)}, a single residence or four-node personal homestead cluster
(such as Lot 402 in the Oasis \texttt{Layer 1b} proving ground) internalizes baseline nourishment,
rainwater harvesting, domestic greywater diversion, solar battery buffering, and basic garage joinery.
Even at this minimal scale, clean-slate Day-1 genesis rapidly expands as the steward ratifies Layer 5
asset policies: staggered crop rotations require separate workflow instances per bed cohort; independent
fermentation crocks, bone-broth simmers, tool maintenance schedules, and somatic rest-hysteresis monitors
bring steady-state concurrency to \textbf{38--68 concurrent workflow instances}, of which \textbf{4--14
tokens} evaluate actively on any given $100\,\mathrm{ms}$ tick across eight physical stations.

In \textbf{Stage 1B (Neighborhood Mesh, 5--12 Lots)}, adjacent households federate their Ring 1
(\texttt{R1}) trust commons while retaining full single-node sovereignty. Each node now hosts not only its
domestic metabolic loops, but also pairwise Bilateral Clearing Channel (\texttt{BCC}) sub-order
fulfillments, shared kiln and joinery tool-lending leases, multi-household childcare or elder check-in
rotations, and Capacitated Vehicle Routing (\texttt{CVRP}) cargo e-trike milk-run handoffs. Single-node
concurrency rises to \textbf{95--185 concurrent workflows}, driving \textbf{12--35 active tokens per
tick}.

In \textbf{Stage 2 (Cooperative Shell, Subchapter T)}, a multi-family cooperative hub or neighborhood
production commons operates a licensed commissary hearth, a CNC/TIG fabrication bay, a propagation
nursery, a subsurface reed-bed greywater train \parencite{kadlec1996}, and a $380\,\mathrm{V}$
\texttt{HVDC} microgrid bus. Because commissary food-safety lots, autoclave sterilization cycles, foundry
melts, and apprentice Bayesian Knowledge Tracing (\texttt{BKT}) capstones \parencite{corbett1995bkt} each
instantiate isolated, auditable token frames, a Stage 2 node sustains \textbf{260--620 concurrent
workflows} with \textbf{35--110 active tokens per tick}.

Finally, in \textbf{Stage 3 (Sovereign Campus, Community Land Trust / Perpetual Purpose Trust)}, a
bioregional campus coordinates silvopasture keyline hydrology, full biorefinery struvite/biogas closure,
open-building infill fabrication \parencite{habraken1972supports}, acute paramedic readiness, and
reproducible software-forge verification. Here a single primary coordinator SBC manages
\textbf{850--2,600+ concurrent workflow instances} and \textbf{110--420 active tokens per tick}, all while
constrained to a $< 5\,\mathrm{W}$ thermal envelope so the orchestrator can ride indefinitely through
multi-week winter overcast on local solar storage.

contiguous RAM---fitting comfortably inside the L2 cache of a commodity embedded processor.

\subsection{Multi-Instance Cohort Isolation and Cultivar Parameterization}

A critical driver of single-node workflow density is \emph{multi-instance parameterization}: running
dozens of concurrent instances of the exact same compiled BPMN bytecode (\texttt{workflow\_def\_cid}) over
distinct physical entities and biological parameters. In early Oasis prototypes, a naive
\texttt{HasWorkflow(wf\_id)} check restricted the node to one global instance per workflow definition,
making it impossible to cultivate four staggered raised beds or ferment six sequential kraut crocks
simultaneously.

In the ratified Layer 4 architecture, \texttt{workflow\_def\_cid} is purely an immutable code segment,
analogous to an ELF \texttt{.text} section, while every spawned instance receives a unique 64-bit
\texttt{instance\_id} bound to a specific physical \texttt{scope\_entity\_id} (such as
\texttt{bed:north\_terrace\_01} vs.\ \texttt{bed:north\_terrace\_02}) and an immutable JSON-LD input
parameter CID \parencite{w3c2020jsonld}. In \texttt{pea\_garden\_and\_soup\_lifecycles.bpmn.json}, the
\texttt{cultivar\_id} input parameter binds \texttt{fabaceae\_cultivars.jsonld} specifications---such as
\texttt{min\_growth\_temp\_mk}, \texttt{target\_min\_vwc\_bp}, \texttt{irrigation\_dose\_ml},
\texttt{fixes\_atmospheric\_n2}, and \texttt{trellis\_ridge\_height\_mm}---directly into L3 state slots
keyed by \texttt{(scope\_entity\_id, prop\_hash)}. Thus Bed 01 can run a fast-maturing \emph{Pisum
sativum} cultivar requiring $1{,}650\,\mathrm{mm}$ trellises alongside Bed 02 running an overwintering
nitrogen-fixing fava cohort, sharing $100\%$ of their compiled WASM instruction pages while maintaining
zero cross-talk across their L3 rhizosphere registers.

\section{Mediated Intra-Node Choreography (Zero Point-to-Point Spaghetti)}
\label{sec:mediated-choreography}

High-density execution would still collapse into software fragility if workflows could reach into one
another's internal state. In conventional object-oriented or actor-spaghetti orchestrators
\parencite{agha1986actors}, a microgrid controller workflow might hold a direct reference to a woodshop
planer workflow and a bone-broth workflow to throttle their power draw, while the soup workflow directly
mutates a garden workflow's harvest counter. In a system of $N$ concurrent workflows, unrestricted
pairwise coupling creates up to $N(N-1)$ stateful interaction channels, destroying modular verification
\parencite{petri1962kommunikation,dennis1966programming} and making deterministic crash replay impossible
whenever arrival order jitters.

Layer 4 eliminates direct inter-workflow coupling at the root. Every workflow instance executes inside an
isolated WebAssembly linear memory sandbox \parencite{haas2017wasm} with zero shared heap pointers, zero
ambient process table visibility, and zero workflow-to-workflow RPC imports. All intra-node coordination
is mediated exclusively through Layer 3 (\texttt{col-storaged}) and Layer 5 (\texttt{col-kmsd}) across
three strictly typed BPMN 2.0 choreography primitives \parencite{omg2011bpmn,chinosi2012bpmn}.

\begin{definition}[The Three Canonical Intra-Node Choreography Channels]
\label{def:three-choreography-channels}
Let $\mathcal{W} = \{W_1, \dots, W_N\}$ be the set of active workflow instances hosted on a single node at
Hybrid Logical Clock epoch $\tau$. Any causal influence from $W_i$ to $W_j$ ($i \neq j$) must traverse one
of three mediated channels:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Layer 3 Broadcast Signals (\texttt{sig:*}):} One-to-many state-predicate facts committed
        to the L3 signal register table under a canonical namespace (e.g.,
        \texttt{sig:solar\_curtailment\_tier3}, \texttt{sig:smoke\_aqi\_breach},
        \texttt{sig:quiet\_hours\_active}). The emitter $W_i$ knows nothing of the subscriber set
        $\mathcal{S}(\texttt{sig:*})$; \texttt{col-storaged} wakes all subscribed catch events and event
        subprocesses atomically at the next tick boundary.
    \item \textbf{Correlated Point-to-Point Message Flows ($\kappa$):} One-to-one asynchronous BPMN
        message envelopes routed via an immutable L3 correlation mailbox indexed by a deterministic
        $256$-bit \texttt{SHA-256} Correlation Key:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:correlation-key}
    \kappa = \mathrm{H}\bigl(\texttt{lot\_cid} \parallel \texttt{stage\_id} \parallel \texttt{epoch\_hlc}\bigr) \in \{0,1\}^{256}
    \end{equation}
    where $\texttt{lot\_cid}$ identifies the content-addressed physical or logical batch artifact,
    $\texttt{stage\_id}$ names the canonical handoff stage, and $\texttt{epoch\_hlc}$ pins the Hybrid
    Logical Clock window. A throwing node in $W_i$ deposits an immutable IPLD payload under $\kappa$;
    exactly the single catching node in $W_j$ registered for $\kappa$ consumes it.
    \item \textbf{Interrupting vs.\ Non-Interrupting BPMN Event Subprocesses:} Embedded reactive scopes
        within a workflow definition \parencite{harel1987statecharts,omg2011bpmn} triggered by a
        \texttt{sig:*} predicate, $\kappa$ message, or L2 telemetry threshold:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item A \emph{non-interrupting} event subprocess (\texttt{is\_interrupting == 0}) sets a secondary
            active bit in the instance's $64$-bit \texttt{active\_tokens} bitset, executing an auxiliary
            adjustment (such as dimming jacket trim power or logging an acoustic warning) while the
            primary process token continues undisturbed.
        \item An \emph{interrupting} event subprocess (\texttt{is\_interrupting == 1}) atomically clears
            all active bits in the enclosing scope's bitset mask, releases Regime 1 digital locks or
            triggers Regime 2 physical salvage parking, and transfers sole execution control to the
            boundary handler.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

    \item \textbf{Linear Wakeup Complexity:} Dispatching $M$ emitted events at tick $t$ requires

\begin{proof}

\end{proof}

\section{Recursive BOM and Stock-Miss Dependency DAGs}
\label{sec:recursive-bom-dags}

While broadcast signals and $\kappa$-correlated messages coordinate active peers, physical production on a
sovereign node is equally driven by \emph{pull-based material deficits}. In the live workflow bundle
\texttt{pea\_garden\_and\_soup\_lifecycles.bpmn.json}, workflows never hardcode procedural calls to spawn
upstream suppliers. Instead, every workflow begins its material phase with an atomic L3 escrow check
(\texttt{L3\_QUERY\_AND\_LOCK\_INVENTORY}) over \texttt{l3\_l4.sock}. When an input lot or tool is
missing, the workflow executes an exclusive gateway branch to an \texttt{IntermediateThrowEvent} that
emits \texttt{YIELD\_UPWARD\_L5} over \texttt{l4\_l5.sock}, parking its token in \texttt{WAITING\_STOCK}
while Layer 5 (\texttt{col-kmsd}) resolves policy-compliant sourcing (\texttt{R0\_LOCAL\_WORKFLOWS},
\texttt{R1\_MESH\_PEERS}, or \texttt{L7\_LEGACY\_CHANNEL}).

\subsection{Tracing a Multi-Hop Intra-Node Stock-Miss Cascade}

Consider what unfolds on a single node when a steward requests evening split-pea potage
(\texttt{need:pea\_soup}) under local \texttt{R0\_LOCAL\_WORKFLOWS} policy before the larder or tool rack
is stocked:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Downstream Culinary Escrow Miss (\texttt{soup\_workflow}):} Layer 5 instantiates

        \texttt{bone\_broth}, and a sanitized kettle (\texttt{clean\_pot}). Finding zero dry peas and an
        unwashed pot, L3 aborts the partial lock (\texttt{escrow\_locked == false}). The workflow emits
        \texttt{YIELD\_UPWARD\_L5} declaring deficits \texttt{need:split\_peas} and
        \texttt{need:clean\_pot}, and suspends cleanly without spinning CPU cycles.
    \item \textbf{Midstream Dish \& Agronomic Instantiation:} Receiving \texttt{YIELD\_UPWARD\_L5} over

    \item \textbf{Nested Hauling-Tool Deficit (\texttt{garden\_workflow}):} As \texttt{garden\_workflow}

    \item \textbf{Upstream Fabrication Resolution (\texttt{garage\_workflow}):} Layer 5 dispatches

    \item \textbf{Bottom-Up Cascade Unblocking:} Crediting \texttt{watering\_can\_tool} fires

When multiple downstream workflows simultaneously encounter deficits for the same precursor---for
instance, when both \texttt{soup\_workflow} (needing $600\,\mathrm{g}$ of dry split peas) and a

batch yield ($1{,}500\,\mathrm{g}$ in \texttt{wf.garden.shell\_dry\_and\_turn\_bed}). Rather than spawning
duplicate redundant plantings, Layer 5 binds both waiting consumers to the single upstream cohort via
fractional L3 reservation claims ($600\,\mathrm{g} + 400\,\mathrm{g} \le 1{,}500\,\mathrm{g}$), turning a
tree of deficits into a true diamond \texttt{DAG}. Only when cumulative downstream demand exceeds the
in-flight batch yield does Layer 5 instantiate a second bed cohort or escalate the residual $R_1$ deficit
to neighborhood peers.

\subsection{The Circular Ecology Paradox and Temporal BOM Cycle-Freedom}

At first glance, recursive \texttt{BOM} expansion raises a profound theoretical objection. Regenerative
agroecology is intentionally \emph{circular} \parencite{odum1971,georgescu1971}: simmering split-pea soup
generates $180\,\mathrm{g}$ of mirepoix peelings (\texttt{peelings\_to\_compost\_g}), which feed
\texttt{BSFL} larvae and thermophilic compost drums (\texttt{wf.compost.thermophilic\_batch}), which
produce \texttt{item.thermophilic\_compost} required by \texttt{wf.garden.plow\_and\_amend\_soil} to grow
the split peas required by \texttt{soup\_workflow}! On a static item-type graph $G_{\mathrm{type}}$, the
cycle
\[
\texttt{pea\_soup} \longrightarrow \texttt{split\_peas} \longrightarrow \texttt{compost} \longrightarrow \texttt{kitchen\_scraps} \longrightarrow \texttt{pea\_soup}
\]
is manifestly cyclic. In a naive orchestrator that resolves stock misses over static item types without
temporal lineage, \texttt{soup\_workflow} waiting on \texttt{garden\_workflow} waiting on
\texttt{compost\_workflow} waiting on scraps from \texttt{soup\_workflow} would form a classic Coffman
circular-wait deadlock \parencite{coffman1971deadlocks}!

Layer 4 and Layer 5 prevent circular metabolic loops from ever inducing execution cycles by indexing every
material dependency over \emph{monotonically advancing Hybrid Logical Clock (\texttt{HLC}) lot cohorts}
\parencite{lamport1978time,kulkarni2014logical}.

\begin{theorem}[Temporal BOM DAG Cycle-Freedom]
\label{thm:temporal-bom-dag}
Let $G_{\mathrm{type}} = (I, E_{\mathrm{type}})$ be a possibly cyclic material transformation schema over
item classes $I$, where metabolic byproducts of downstream processes regenerate inputs for upstream
processes. In the Sovereign Stack, every workflow instance $W \in \mathcal{W}$ is parameterized by a
strictly bounded target realization epoch $\tau_{\mathrm{due}}(W)$ in the totally ordered HLC domain
$(\mathcal{T}, <_{\mathrm{HLC}})$, and every physical inventory lot $\ell$ committed to Layer 3 carries an
immutable creation stamp $\tau_{\mathrm{born}}(\ell) \in \mathcal{T}$. Under the \textbf{Causal Precursor
Sourcing Rule}---which mandates that any upstream sub-workflow $W_{\mathrm{up}}$ spawned to satisfy a
deficit of $W_{\mathrm{down}}$ must satisfy
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:causal-precursor-rule}
\tau_{\mathrm{now}} <_{\mathrm{HLC}} \tau_{\mathrm{due}}(W_{\mathrm{up}}) + \Delta_{\min}(W_{\mathrm{down}}) \le_{\mathrm{HLC}} \tau_{\mathrm{due}}(W_{\mathrm{down}})
\end{equation}
with strictly positive minimum transformation lead time $\Delta_{\min}(W_{\mathrm{down}}) > 0$, falling
back to committed stock $\tau_{\mathrm{born}}(\ell) \le_{\mathrm{HLC}} \tau_{\mathrm{now}}$ or external
\texttt{R1\_MESH} / \texttt{L7\_FALLBACK} genesis inputs whenever remaining lead time is
insufficient---the live runtime dependency graph $G_{\mathrm{BOM}} = (\mathcal{W}, E_{\mathrm{wait}})$ is
a finite Directed Acyclic Graph (\texttt{DAG}) at every tick.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose toward a contradiction that at some runtime tick with current clock $\tau_{\mathrm{now}}$, the
active wait-for relation $E_{\mathrm{wait}}$ contains a directed cycle of suspended workflow instances:
\[
W_0 \xrightarrow{\text{waits}} W_1 \xrightarrow{\text{waits}} W_2 \xrightarrow{\text{waits}} \cdots \xrightarrow{\text{waits}} W_k = W_0 \qquad (k \ge 1),
\]
where $(W_r, W_{r+1}) \in E_{\mathrm{wait}}$ denotes that $W_r$ emitted \texttt{YIELD\_UPWARD\_L5} and is
blocked awaiting a future output lot to be produced by $W_{r+1}$. By the Causal Precursor Sourcing Rule
(Equation~\ref{eq:causal-precursor-rule}), every edge $(W_r, W_{r+1}) \in E_{\mathrm{wait}}$ enforces:
\[
\tau_{\mathrm{due}}(W_{r+1}) \le_{\mathrm{HLC}} \tau_{\mathrm{due}}(W_r) - \Delta_{\min}(W_r).
\]
Because every physical transformation requires at least one $100\,\mathrm{ms}$ orchestrator tick
($\Delta_{\min}(W_r) \ge 1\text{ tick} > 0$), we obtain the strict inequality
$\tau_{\mathrm{due}}(W_{r+1}) <_{\mathrm{HLC}} \tau_{\mathrm{due}}(W_r)$ for every $0 \le r < k$. By
transitivity of the strict total order $<_{\mathrm{HLC}}$ on 64-bit Hybrid Logical Clocks:
\[
\tau_{\mathrm{due}}(W_0) = \tau_{\mathrm{due}}(W_k) <_{\mathrm{HLC}} \cdots <_{\mathrm{HLC}} \tau_{\mathrm{due}}(W_1) <_{\mathrm{HLC}} \tau_{\mathrm{due}}(W_0),
\]
contradicting irreflexivity ($\tau_{\mathrm{due}}(W_0) \not<_{\mathrm{HLC}} \tau_{\mathrm{due}}(W_0)$).
Moreover, since $\tau_{\mathrm{due}}(W_r) >_{\mathrm{HLC}} \tau_{\mathrm{now}}$ and each hop decrements
the finite integer HLC slack $\tau_{\mathrm{due}} - \tau_{\mathrm{now}}$ by at least $\Delta_{\min} \ge
1$, any recursive \texttt{YIELD\_UPWARD\_L5} chain terminates in at most $\lfloor
(\tau_{\mathrm{due}}(W_0) - \tau_{\mathrm{now}}) / \min \Delta_{\min} \rfloor$ steps, where the leaf
deficit either locks already-existing Cohort $(g-1)$ compost ($\tau_{\mathrm{born}}(\ell)
\le_{\mathrm{HLC}} \tau_{\mathrm{now}}$) or routes to \texttt{R1\_MESH} / \texttt{L7\_FALLBACK} genesis
bootstrap. Hence $G_{\mathrm{BOM}}$ is finite and acyclic.
\end{proof}

Operationally, Theorem~\ref{thm:temporal-bom-dag} unrolls circular ecology into a spiral helix across
generations: Cohort $g$ soup consumes Cohort $g$ peas grown in Cohort $(g-1)$ compost derived from Cohort
$(g-1)$ soup scraps, while Day-1 genesis bootstraps Cohort $0$ compost via neighborhood \texttt{R1\_MESH}
surplus offers (such as North-Oak Commons' thermophilic compost drums) or \texttt{L7\_FALLBACK} organic
matter.

\subsection{Priority Inheritance Across Dependency Edges}

Acyclic \texttt{BOM} graphs prevent deadlock, but without priority propagation they remain vulnerable to
\emph{priority inversion} \parencite{liu1973scheduling}. Suppose a Tier 1 (\texttt{B\_baseline})
postpartum hearth workflow requires a clean tiffin carrier or a replacement silicone gasket fabricated by
a garage workflow whose intrinsic domain classification is Tier 3 (\texttt{Discretionary}). If a burst of
Tier 2 (\texttt{MutualAid}) neighborhood jobs saturates the workshop queue or exergy budget, the Tier 3
fabrication task would starve, indirectly stalling the Tier 1 baseline care meal!

To eliminate priority inversion across decoupled workflows without letting \texttt{col-execd} invent
policy, Layer 4 implements deterministic \textbf{Dynamic Priority Inheritance} over $G_{\mathrm{BOM}}$:

\begin{invariant}[DAG Priority Inheritance Monotonicity]
\label{inv:priority-inheritance}
Let $P_0(W) \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4\}$ denote the intrinsic constitutional priority tier assigned by Layer 5
at instantiation ($0 = \texttt{F\_eco}$, $1 = \texttt{B\_baseline}$, $2 = \texttt{MutualAid}$, $3 =
\texttt{Discretionary}$, $4 = \texttt{L7\_Export}$, where numerically smaller integers denote higher
urgency). Whenever an active wait edge $(W_{\mathrm{downstream}}, W_{\mathrm{upstream}}) \in
E_{\mathrm{wait}}$ exists in $G_{\mathrm{BOM}}$, the effective scheduling priority
$P_{\mathrm{eff}}(W_{\mathrm{upstream}})$ is updated recursively across all ancestor edges:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:priority-inheritance}
P_{\mathrm{eff}}(W_{\mathrm{upstream}}) \leftarrow \min\Bigl(\, P_0(W_{\mathrm{upstream}}),\; \min_{(W_d,\, W_{\mathrm{upstream}}) \in E_{\mathrm{wait}}} P_{\mathrm{eff}}(W_d) \,\Bigr).
\end{equation}
Immediately upon L3 settlement of the awaited lot $\ell$ (deleting the edge from $E_{\mathrm{wait}}$),
$P_{\mathrm{eff}}(W_{\mathrm{upstream}})$ recomputes from its remaining active dependents, reverting to
$P_0(W_{\mathrm{upstream}})$ when no higher-priority downstream workflow awaits it.
\end{invariant}

enum class PriorityTier : uint8_t {
    TIER_0_ECO_FLOOR     = 0, // Ecological & life-safety invariants
    TIER_1_BASELINE_CARE = 1, // R0 nutrition, potable water, perinatal/elder care
    TIER_2_MUTUAL_AID    = 2, // R1 neighborhood treaty & BCC commitments
    TIER_3_DISCRETIONARY = 3, // Local comfort, craft upgrades, non-urgent fab
    TIER_4_L7_EXPORT     = 4  // Bill-offset surplus commissions
};

struct alignas(8) BomWaitEdge {
    uint32_t downstream_wf_idx{0U}; // Blocked consumer workflow index
    uint32_t upstream_wf_idx{0U};   // Producer workflow index
};

inline void RecomputeEffectivePriorities(
    uint8_t* effective_tier,
    const uint8_t* intrinsic_tier,
    uint32_t wf_count,
    const BomWaitEdge* edges,
    uint32_t edge_count
) noexcept {
    for (uint32_t i = 0U; i < wf_count; ++i) {
        effective_tier[i] = intrinsic_tier[i];
    }
    // Since G_BOM is a proven finite DAG (Theorem 4.2), relaxation converges
    // in at most dag_depth <= wf_count passes (typically 2..4 passes).
    for (uint32_t pass = 0U; pass < wf_count; ++pass) {
        bool changed = false;
        for (uint32_t e = 0U; e < edge_count; ++e) {
            const uint32_t down = edges[e].downstream_wf_idx;
            const uint32_t up   = edges[e].upstream_wf_idx;
            if (effective_tier[down] < effective_tier[up]) {
                effective_tier[up] = effective_tier[down];
                changed = true;
            }
        }
        if (!changed) {
            break;
        }
    }
}
\end{lstlisting}

Because Theorem~\ref{thm:temporal-bom-dag} guarantees that $G_{\mathrm{BOM}}$ is acyclic and shallow (in
practice, recursive \texttt{BOM} chains rarely exceed depth $4$ or $5$),

also the strict constitutional boundary: if elevating a Tier 3 workshop workflow to Tier 1

loads, \texttt{col-execd} only inherits scheduling priority for tasks explicitly linked by Layer 5's

never spoof an upward dependency to pilfer protected exergy, and single-node density scales cleanly to

Layer 5 (\texttt{col-kmsd}) and Layer 6 (\texttt{col-commonsd}) decompile this machine frame upward ($\Pi_{\mathrm{up}}^{\mathrm{diff}} : \mathrm{L4} \to \mathrm{L5} \to \mathrm{L6}$) into a human-readable \textbf{Layer 6 Semantic Diff Card} while simultaneously querying \texttt{col-meshd} across Ring $R_1$ for a neighboring capability lease, as detailed in Chapter~\ref{ch:internode-mesh}.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{lstlisting}[language=C++, label={lst:bpmn-hardware-cascade-v50}, caption={Production C++20 structures and deterministic evaluator for the 3-Tier BPMN Hardware Adaptation Cascade (\texttt{CCI-5}, \texttt{D8}).}]
#pragma once
#include <cstdint>
#include <array>

namespace collective::layer4 {

enum class HardwareFleetTier : uint8_t {
    kScavengedLayer7Shimmed = 0, // Stage I: Proprietary brain + external L2 shim (high ERC & blind faults)
    kRightToRepairHybrid    = 1, // Stage II: Scavenged heavy chassis + open L2 sensor/actuator brain
    kNativeOpenSpec         = 2  // Stage III: Open-spec mechanics + full predictive L2 twin
};

enum class BpmnCascadeResolution : uint8_t {
    kDirectExecution              = 0,
    kTier1SiParameterScaled       = 1, // Scaled batch count, feed rate, or thermal ramp duration in SI units
    kTier2MacroActivitySpliced    = 2, // Substituted functionally equivalent sub-workflow from Ontological Graph
    kTier3TrueConstraintHardBlock = 3  // Physical law / capability deficit -> decompile to L6 Semantic Diff
};

struct ToolTwinCapabilityV50 {
    std::array<uint8_t, 32> tool_did_hash;
    HardwareFleetTier fleet_tier{HardwareFleetTier::kScavengedLayer7Shimmed};
    int64_t max_volume_ml{10'000};                 // Canonical milliliters (mL)
    int64_t max_power_mw{1'800'000};               // Canonical milliwatts (mW)
    int64_t max_temp_mk{523'150};                  // Canonical millikelvins (523.15 K = 250.0 C)
    int64_t parasitic_shim_overhead_mw{45'000};    // Extra standby/shim power draw for Stage I hardware
    int64_t erc_amortized_per_cycle_uj{850'000'000LL}; // Layer 3 Ecological Replacement Cost per cycle (uJ)
    int64_t unscheduled_fault_prob_bp{350};        // Blind hard-block rate in basis points (Stage I: 3.50%, Stage III: 5 bp)
    bool has_macro_substitute_available{false};
    std::array<uint8_t, 32> macro_substitute_cid{};
};

struct BpmnTaskRequirementV50 {
    int64_t required_volume_ml{20'000};
    int64_t required_thermal_energy_uj{7'200'000'000LL};
    int64_t required_temp_mk{503'150};             // 230.0 C
    bool allow_batch_splitting{true};
};

struct BpmnCascadeOutcomeV50 {
    BpmnCascadeResolution resolution{BpmnCascadeResolution::kDirectExecution};
    int64_t scaled_sub_batch_count{1};
    int64_t scaled_duration_ms{0};
    int64_t total_exergy_with_erc_uj{0};
    int64_t temp_deficit_mk{0};                    // Non-zero only on Tier 3 True-Constraint Hard-Block
};

[[nodiscard]] constexpr BpmnCascadeOutcomeV50 EvaluateBpmnHardwareCascadeV50(
    const BpmnTaskRequirementV50& req,
    const ToolTwinCapabilityV50& tool) noexcept {
    BpmnCascadeOutcomeV50 out{};
    // Check fundamental intensive thermodynamic limit (temperature ceiling)
    if (req.required_temp_mk > tool.max_temp_mk) {
        if (tool.has_macro_substitute_available) {
            out.resolution = BpmnCascadeResolution::kTier2MacroActivitySpliced;
            return out;
        }
        out.resolution = BpmnCascadeResolution::kTier3TrueConstraintHardBlock;
        out.temp_deficit_mk = req.required_temp_mk - tool.max_temp_mk;
        return out;
    }
    // Tier 1: SI Parameter Scaling across volume (mL) and power (mW -> duration ms)
    int64_t batches = 1;
    if (req.required_volume_ml > tool.max_volume_ml) {
        if (!req.allow_batch_splitting) {
            out.resolution = tool.has_macro_substitute_available
                ? BpmnCascadeResolution::kTier2MacroActivitySpliced
                : BpmnCascadeResolution::kTier3TrueConstraintHardBlock;
            return out;
        }
        batches = (req.required_volume_ml + tool.max_volume_ml - 1) / tool.max_volume_ml;
    }
    // Power in mW = 10^-3 J/s = 10^3 uJ/s = 1 uJ/ms -> duration_ms = energy_uj / power_mw
    const int64_t single_batch_duration_ms =
        (tool.max_power_mw > 0) ? (req.required_thermal_energy_uj / tool.max_power_mw) : 0;
    out.scaled_sub_batch_count = batches;
    out.scaled_duration_ms = batches * single_batch_duration_ms;
    const int64_t shim_waste_uj = tool.parasitic_shim_overhead_mw * out.scaled_duration_ms;
    out.total_exergy_with_erc_uj =
        (batches * req.required_thermal_energy_uj) + shim_waste_uj + (batches * tool.erc_amortized_per_cycle_uj);
    out.resolution = (batches > 1)
        ? BpmnCascadeResolution::kTier1SiParameterScaled
        : BpmnCascadeResolution::kDirectExecution;
    return out;
}

\subsection{Co-Located Enveloping Node Choreography Across Shared Domestic Partitions}

Resolving a task's internal tool capability via \texttt{EvaluateBpmnHardwareCascadeV50} is necessary but not sufficient when multiple autonomous Layer 5 nodes coexist inside the same physical structure (\texttt{CCI-4}, \texttt{D5}, \texttt{D6}). Consider a single residence hosting three overlapping polycentric nodes beneath one roof:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Child Node A --- The Maker Bench ($N_s = 1$):} A solo fabrication node in the ground-floor workshop bay operating a CNC router and joinery planer rated at $L_{\mathrm{spindle}} = 750\,\mathrm{cB}$ ($75.0\,\mathrm{dBA}$) and $P_{\mathrm{spindle}} = 2{,}200{,}000\,\mathrm{mW}$ ($2.2\,\mathrm{kW}$).

    \item \textbf{Child Node C --- The Household Hearth ($N_s = 4$):} The shared kitchen and living commons drawing up to $6{,}000{,}000\,\mathrm{mW}$ ($6.0\,\mathrm{kW}$) across induction stockpots, water heaters, and laundry loops.
\end{itemize}

The House Enveloping Node knows nothing of the Maker Bench's G-code toolpaths, the Solo Studio's Scenario Zero RFC backlog, or the Hearth's pea-soup recipe. It governs strictly the shared physical partition invariants (\texttt{EnvelopingBoundaryLeaseV50}): acoustic flux in centibels ($\mathrm{cB}$), instantaneous feeder draw in milliwatts ($\mathrm{mW}$), window exergy in microjoules ($\mu\mathrm{J}$), thermal rejection ($\mathrm{mK}$), and mandatory finite lease TTLs (\texttt{lease\_ttl\_ms}).

Before \texttt{col-execd} issues a Layer 2 \texttt{ActuationEnvelopeV1} to spin up the Maker Bench's $750\,\mathrm{cB}$ CNC spindle at \texttt{13:20}, it executes a deterministic four-step boundary choreography loop across \texttt{l3\_l4.sock} and \texttt{l4\_l5.sock}:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Boundary Lease Intersection Check:} \texttt{col-execd} queries Layer 3 (\texttt{col-storaged}) for all active \texttt{EnvelopingBoundaryLeaseV50} records bound to the House Enveloping Node. It detects that the Solo Studio holds an active quiet-hours recording lease with ceiling $L_{\max,\mathrm{env}} = 420\,\mathrm{cB}$ ($42.0\,\mathrm{dBA}$) expiring in $38\,\mathrm{minutes}$ ($\texttt{lease\_ttl\_ms} = 2{,}280{,}000\,\mathrm{ms}$), and that the House feeder breaker cap ($9{,}600{,}000\,\mathrm{mW}$) currently has $5{,}800{,}000\,\mathrm{mW}$ committed to the Hearth's simmer kettles.

\end{enumerate}

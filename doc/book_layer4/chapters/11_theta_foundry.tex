\chapter{Scenario Theta: The Extraction \& Refinement Foundry}
\label{ch:theta}

\section{The Legacy Paradigm \& Its Failures}
The legacy industrial supply chain deliberately abstracts the catastrophic thermodynamic and ecological costs of material refinement away from the end consumer. Raw materials are brutally extracted via strip mining in one hemisphere, refined in massive, centralized, heavily polluting fabs in another, and shipped globally via diesel logistics networks. Localized recycling is largely a performative ``wish-cycling'' charade, where municipal waste is aggregated and shipped to developing nations to be landfilled or burned.

While decentralizing industrial refinement (smelting aluminum, extruding HDPE plastics, cutting granite, doping silicon) to the neighborhood scale creates true sovereign resilience, it introduces severe localized risks. Foundries require extreme thermal exergy ($>1000^\circ\text{C}$), produce highly toxic volatile organic compounds (VOCs), and draw massive electrical loads. Operating such machinery for single, isolated jobs is thermodynamically disastrous and violates the core principles of exergy conservation. Without rigorous algorithmic batching and safety controls, a neighborhood foundry becomes a hazardous, inefficient liability.

\section{The Incremental Automation Pathway}
Integrating heavy industrial processing into a localized Node requires extreme caution and a slow progression from manual oversight to automated batching.

\subsection{Phase 1: Human-in-the-Loop (Manual Orchestration)}
In the early stages, the foundry is operated manually by master craftsmen and certified Stewards. Citizens submit \texttt{RefinementBounties} (e.g., requests to cast a bronze part or extrude filament) via a basic ledger. The Steward manually reviews the queue, decides when enough raw material (scrap) has been collected to justify firing the kiln, and coordinates the operation. Telemetry (kiln temperature, exhaust ventilation) is monitored via analog gauges. Safety relies entirely on the Steward's physical presence and subjective judgment regarding air quality and municipal noise compliance.

\subsection{Phase 2: The Centaur (AI-Assisted)}
The Orchestrator begins to ingest Layer 2 telemetry from the foundry's IoT sensors (Type-K thermocouples, PM2.5 monitors). When a user submits a \texttt{RefinementBounty}, the AI cross-references the request against the current scrap inventory and calculates the required exergy footprint. The AI groups compatible jobs into optimal batches and suggests a firing schedule based on local grid load and AQI forecasts. However, the Orchestrator cannot actuate the kiln. The human Steward must physically verify the crucible contents, check the safety gear, and manually initiate the high-voltage relays.

\subsection{Phase 3: Full Autonomy}
The Orchestrator achieves full batching and safety autonomy. It pools incoming \texttt{RefinementBounties} in a suspended state machine. The kiln is \textit{only} authorized to fire when the queue reaches an optimal exergy threshold (e.g., 85\% crucible capacity). Layer 2 sensors continuously stream VOC, PM2.5, and thermal data to the Orchestrator. If exhaust levels spike or thermal limits are breached, the Orchestrator autonomously triggers emergency hardware relays, cutting power and aborting the BPMN sequence. The Orchestrator strictly enforces Layer 5 credentials, cryptographically locking the machinery unless a Steward with a verified \texttt{Foundry\_Safety\_L3} credential is authenticated at the terminal.

\section{The Human Boundary (Limits of Automation)}
The Orchestrator's power over the foundry is absolute regarding data, batching, and emergency cutoff, but it cannot manipulate the physical molten state. The AI can calculate the phase change of aluminum at $660^\circ\text{C}$ and actuate the kiln's electrical elements, but it cannot physically pour the crucible. The highly dangerous, dexterous act of transferring liquid metal into a green-sand mold, or safely handling concentrated acid baths for e-waste etching, remains strictly a human operation.

Furthermore, the AI cannot bypass the ``Last Resort'' virgin extraction gate through logic alone. If the community requires raw copper and the scrap ledger is empty, the Orchestrator will calculate the immense Ecological Replacement Cost (ERC) penalty for virgin mining. It will not authorize the extraction; it merely presents the thermodynamic cost. The human Trust Ring must consciously choose to pay that exergy penalty and accept the ecological debt.

\section{Orchestration Mechanics (The ``How'')}
Scenario Theta pushes the Orchestrator's thermodynamic cellular automata and queueing models to their physical limits.

\textbf{Exergy Aggregation and Batch Queuing:} The Orchestrator models the foundry requests using a bulk-service queueing model. Incoming \texttt{RefinementBounties} are not processed sequentially; they are held in a \texttt{Parallel Gateway} within the BPMN engine. The workflow remains in a suspended state, evaluating a \texttt{Conditional Event} loop against the \texttt{target\_mass\_grams} variable. Only when the aggregated mass of the queued jobs exceeds the calculated efficiency threshold (e.g., $>85\%$ of the kiln's capacity) does the BPMN engine transition state, releasing the aggregated batch to the authorized Steward and escrowing the massive exergy cost from the requesters' wallets.

\textbf{Extreme Heat Diffusion in Voxel Automata:} Within the digital twin (Oasis engine), the foundry operates at extreme temperatures. The \texttt{ChunkManager} must execute high-frequency thermal diffusion algorithms. If the \texttt{FOUNDRY\_KILN} voxel reaches operating temperature, the engine calculates the thermal bleed into adjacent voxels based on their \texttt{thermal\_resistance} properties. If a user places the kiln adjacent to a \texttt{WOOD\_WALL} voxel, the engine mathematically determinates a combustion event, transitioning the wall to a \texttt{FIRE} state and cascading the failure, heavily penalizing poor spatial design.

\textbf{Phase Change Logic and Real-Time L2 Cutoff:} The Orchestrator tracks the specific heat capacity of \texttt{SOLID} item entities within the kiln voxel. Upon crossing the precise melting point (e.g., $660^\circ\text{C}$ for aluminum), the engine triggers a state transition to \texttt{LIQUID}, unlocking the \texttt{POUR} action for the authenticated human player. Simultaneously, the Orchestrator runs a high-priority, low-latency asynchronous loop monitoring the Layer 2 VOC and PM2.5 exhaust sensors. If the telemetry detects a spike exceeding municipal safety thresholds (e.g., due to burning plastic or improper ventilation), the Orchestrator bypasses standard queuing, instantly triggering an emergency relay cutoff to the kiln's power supply and transitioning the BPMN state machine to \texttt{FAILED\_EMISSIONS}.

# Lore & Continuity Critique: Chapter 2

## Overview
The chapter establishes a strong narrative connecting the present-day physical/digital integration with the painful lessons of the "System Call" era. However, it currently misses opportunities to weave in established continuity characters from the previous book, *System Call*. 

## Character Continuity Checks

### 1. Kael (Elara's Mentor / First Meal)
**Current State:** Kael is absent from the text. When Elara vouches for David and Sarah, she says, *"Because ten years ago, someone did it for me"* (Line 143). Furthermore, during the flashback to the first Key-Signing Ceremony, the text mentions sharing "meager rations of rice and beans" without attributing who started the tradition.
**Recommendation:** This is the perfect emotional anchor to reference Kael. Change "someone" to "Kael" in her dialogue. Additionally, explicitly mention that Kael offered her the first meal of rice and beans during that freezing November night. This reinforces his mentorship and honors the established lore of who initiated the protocol.

### 2. Jax (Engineer of the Initial Approach)
**Current State:** Jax is absent. The text mentions "brilliant cryptographers and network architects" (Line 59) and a man named "Jonas" who broke the node (Line 65). Elara is seen trying to splice a severed fiber-optic cable alone (Line 63).
**Recommendation:** Integrate Jax into the mud and rain flashback scene. Instead of Elara kneeling in the mud alone, she could be working alongside Jax to splice the cable or fix the hardware. For example, *"Elara remembered kneeling in the mud outside the half-built Gamma Kitchen... trying to splice a severed fiber-optic cable while Jax frantically tried to keep the initial approach from failing."* This properly credits Jax's engineering role during the Genesis Protocol.

### 3. Vain (Antagonist / Avatar of the Legacy System)
**Current State:** Vain is not mentioned. The "legacy world" and "legacy architecture" are treated as abstract, systemic forces rather than having a specific avatar.
**Recommendation:** While Vain might not need a physical appearance in this specific settlement ingestion scene, referencing Vain's influence would solidify the threat. When David mentions his cracked phone or the "algorithmic gig dispatchers" (Line 83), or when referring to the "dying empire outside" (Line 167), a subtle nod to Vain's overarching architecture or the shadow of Vain's systems would tie the systemic oppression back to the established antagonist.

## Conclusion
The continuity holds conceptually, but the specific names are currently ignored. By making minor adjustments to the flashback scenes and dialogue, the chapter can smoothly respect the legacy of *System Call* without over-encumbering the current narrative.

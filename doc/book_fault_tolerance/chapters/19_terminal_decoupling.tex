\chapter{Terminal Decoupling}

For thirty-six hours Furnace One never shut down.

Working on Auxiliary Wheel Two plus every amp the south solar panels could pull through April cloud-breaks, Foundry crews poured twelve-hundred-pound concrete-and-slag road pyramids, bolted double polycarbonate shields over the clinic skylights, and welded secondary steel bars across the western gate rams.

They were not the only ones moving before Friday dawn.

Forty miles east inside the metro grid, Priya's mobile crews came up out of the storm drains at four in the morning, not with Molotovs, which tactical squads knew how to shoot on sight, but with sixty cargo trikes hauling steaming fifty-gallon soup cauldrons and sourdough crates straight across the main arterial approaches to Interstate Ninety. By five-thirty, four thousand hungry apartment residents sat ten-deep across six lanes of pavement eating hot barley broth outside the central precinct garages, stalling two entire riot-transport companies bumper-to-bumper without a single rock thrown. Out across the southern farm counties, organized tractor mechanics pulled main distributor rotors on twenty contracted equipment haulers, while inside District Four's central utility bunker, two night-shift dispatchers padlocked the high-voltage backfeed disconnects and logged the keys lost in transit.

Even so, Blackwood Siding needed no highway bridges to reach Elara's gate. Twelve miles of dry county blacktop connected Brenner's rail spur straight to the western turnoff of County Road Nine.

Dawn came up flat slate-gray and windless at thirty-nine degrees.

Right across the faded yellow centerline where valley trust ground met County Road Nine stood four hundred and eighty people five ranks deep, arms hooked elbow-to-elbow through heavy canvas coats and oiled wool sweaters. Nobody carried a club, a blade, or a firearm. Behind them in the second medical row stood Lin, Tomas, and one-handed Tobin beside two electric handcarts stacked with vinegar-soaked bandanas, saline squirt bottles, and plywood splints. Four places down from Doran in the third rank stood Marek and six soot-stained stokers from Lodge Four, coats buttoned high and mouths grim; whatever hadn't healed inside the valley since February wasn't getting settled by Meridian's bulldozers.

At the center of the front rank stood Elara with Silas on her right, seventeen-year-old Gabe on her left, and Jace planted directly behind Gabe's shoulder in the second line, chewing an unlit birch twig at the corner of his mouth while flexing stiff fingers inside fingerless wool mitts.

And linked arm-in-arm across the right-hand lane stood Tahoma and twenty-four tribal councilors, grandmothers, and young treaty runners who had driven down off the reservation ridge roads at three that morning. An elder woman in a cedar-bark hat tended a small cast-iron brazier set dead center on the painted boundary stripe, sending a thin vertical thread of white sage and sweetgrass smoke up into the cold air. Tahoma carried no weapon either, only an eagle-feather staff in his left hand and the beaded buckskin tube holding the duplicate 1854 treaty parchment against his chest.

"State deeds on this creek aren't two centuries old," Tahoma said quietly to Gabe, noticing the boy's rapid breathing in the gray light. "Salmon and cedar knew our names here ten thousand winters back. Keep your heels flat, nephew."

Half a mile east toward the Blackwood rail turnoff, diesel smoke boiled black above the alders.

Twelve armored personnel carriers rolled around the bend in two columns, heavy steel ram-plows bolted low across their bumpers. Marching behind the lead six state-police rigs came two hundred county and state patrol troopers in black polycarbonate shin guards, chest plates, and scratched riot visors.

Marching separately on the north gravel shoulder behind four unmarked slate-gray six-by-sixes, however, came sixty men who didn't walk like local highway patrolmen. Dressed in charcoal flame-resistant flight suits, high-cut ballistic helmets without badge numbers, and wraparound dark goggles, they carried forty-millimeter multi-shot drum launchers and heavy steel-core polymer truncheons: Meridian's contracted asset-recovery unit, brought in off out-of-state rail cars through Brenner's siding at fifty dollars an hour hard scrip.

One hundred yards short of the sage brazier, air brakes hissed and twelve diesels idled rough and loud, vibrating loose gravel against Elara's boot soles.

For ninety seconds nobody spoke except cedar crackling softly in the iron pan.

Then roof horns on the lead state command carrier blared metallic and flat across the wet pastures:

\begin{center}
\textbf{ATTENTION ALL PERSONS BLOCKING COUNTY ROAD NINE.}\\
\textbf{PURSUANT TO EMERGENCY RECLAMATION WRIT SEVEN-TWELVE, ALL CORPORATE AND TRUST EXEMPTIONS ON THIS PARCEL STAND REVOKED.}\\
\textbf{DISPERSE FROM THE ROADWAY IMMEDIATELY.}\\
\textbf{FAILURE TO CLEAR WITHIN SIXTY SECONDS AUTHORIZES CHEMICAL AND KINETIC DISPERSAL.}
\end{center}

Beside Elara, Gabe swallowed dryly once, then locked his left elbow tighter through hers. Directly behind them, Jace spat his chewed birch twig onto the asphalt and rested one steady palm briefly between Gabe's shoulder blades: "Chin tucked when the cans pop, kid. Breathe through wet cloth."

"Look straight through their plastic shields when they close!" Elara called down the five-hundred-person line so both sides of the asphalt heard her clear. "Half the troopers in that left column have cousins in Duvall and River Landing! Don't swing a single hand back. Make them look at the faces they're hitting!"

An air horn blasted short.

Pop-guns thumped hollow off the carrier roofs. Sixteen gray aluminum canisters arced high over the pavement and clattered spinning ten yards ahead of the brazier, vomiting dense yellow-white CS and pepper fog straight across the road on the morning draft.

First inhalation felt like swallowing boiling battery acid.

Elara's eyelids slammed shut involuntarily as fire tore down her windpipe into her lungs; she bent double coughing hot bile against her sleeve while tears poured scalding tracks through the soot on her cheeks. Instantly Lin and Tobin shoved wet cider-vinegar rags up into their palms from behind while Silas's right hand stayed clamped like a bench vise around Elara's upper arm so the rank wouldn't part.

"Move! Clear the blacktop!" a state lieutenant barked through a megaphone.

Out of the yellow fog crashed two hundred riot shields.

Heavy Lexan slammed straight into collarbones, chins, and locked forearms. Up and down the center stripe, wood and plastic boomed dull and wet against wool coats.

A two-hundred-and-thirty-pound state trooper drove his shield rim full-tilt into Silas's chest. Silas slid backward eighteen inches on frosted gravel, dropped his center of gravity, and hooked both bare hands over the top lip of the Lexan shield, locking the man's charge dead still.

From the diagonal gap to their right, a second state patrolman swung a heavy polycarbonate baton overhead and brought it down full force across Silas's left collarbone.

Bone snapped loud enough that Elara felt the jar right through Silas's right elbow.

Silas dropped onto his left knee with a short grunt, left arm hanging dead against his coat buttons. Instead of letting go or swinging a fist, he shot his right hand straight out through the smoke, grabbed the webbing on the patrolman's chest rig, and yanked him six inches closer to his face.

"Miller!" Silas rasped through bloody spit, squinting raw red eyes straight through the man's scratched faceplate. "I know that taped watchband, Dan Miller! You and me winched your brother's logging rig out of South Fork floodwater three Aprils back! Your little girl Maya goes to clinic in Duvall for her lungs! Look through that damn glass at who you're breaking!"

Behind the fogged polycarbonate visor, Dan Miller's eyes went wide white. His raised baton shook violently at shoulder height and stopped dead.

"Silas?" came a muffled croak through the respirator valve.

"Push through that seam, Miller!" a sergeant yelled three yards back.

Miller didn't swing again; his arm sagged slowly down against his thigh, boots rooted to the yellow paint.

Twenty yards right on the reservation flank, eight troopers stopped three paces short of the smoking sage brazier where Tahoma and three grandmothers held the eagle staff and buckskin treaty tube chest-high while singing an old coast paddle song straight through coughing lungs. The point trooper, a twenty-two-year-old kid from the lower river reserve in state blue, stared at the eagle feathers for four seconds, unclipped his chin-strap with shaking thumbs, dropped both helmet and shield clattering onto the road, and knelt squarely behind Tahoma in the smoke.

All across the left lane, county and state patrol ranks were bogging down into shoving matches they had no stomach to finish.

Up on the hood of the gray six-by-six on the north shoulder, Meridian's contract commander saw the state line stalling before any appellate call could clear the capital.

"State boys are choking!" his amplified handset barked harsh across the gravel ditch. "Wedge Two, push left-center seam! Clear that gatehouse now!"

Sixty badge-less men in charcoal suits didn't hesitate and didn't know a soul in three counties. Forming a tight flying wedge behind reinforced breach shields, they charged straight across the north ditch into the coughing seam beside Gabe and Doran, firing forty-millimeter hard-sponge kinetic baton rounds point-blank at ten yards instead of arcing them high.

\textit{Thump-thump-thump.}

A forty-millimeter kinetic slug caught Doran square across the ribs, folding him sideways into Marek's arms; the second round hit seventeen-year-old Gabe dead center in the sternum with the force of a swung sledgehammer.

Air exploded out of Gabe's lungs in a spray of pink spit. His boots left the asphalt completely, slamming him flat onto his back on the torn tar with fractured ribs locking his chest paralyzed while his ears rang deafening white.

Out of the yellow tear-gas cloud five feet above Gabe's head burst three gray-suited Meridian contractors at a dead run, steel-core truncheons raised two-handed to cave in the downed boy's skull and split the road wide open toward the gatehouse.

Gabe tried to roll onto his elbow and couldn't draw breath to move.

From half a step behind him, Jace lunged straight forward over Gabe's body onto both knees on the pavement, planting his lean, tattooed frame like a timber bridge directly over the boy's chest and head.

"Stay flat under me, Papa!" Jace barked down into Gabe's ringing ear, throwing both bare, corded forearms up to lock around the lead contractor's shin guards and stop six boots from stomping Gabe's ribs.

The lead contractor cursed and swung his steel-core truncheon straight down with both hands across the base of Jace's bare neck. A second contractor on the flank swung crosswise into Jace's left temple, and from four feet back a drum-launcher boomed flat in the fog, driving a point-blank kinetic slug square behind Jace's ear.

Gabe felt all three impacts shudder heavy and awful down through Jace's shoulders above him, and still Jace didn't collapse flat onto Gabe's broken ribs; locking both elbows rigid into the asphalt beside Gabe's ears on sheer dying muscle memory, Jace stayed arched three inches clear for two more seconds until blood dripped warm off his nose onto Gabe's chin and his eyes went fixed and quiet toward the orchard trees behind them.

"JACE!" Elara's scream tore raw across the smoke as Tarn and Marek surged out of the third rank like madmen, not swinging iron, locking bare arms around two contractors' shoulders and dragging both men bodily off into the ditch mud while one-armed Silas hauled Jace and Gabe backward behind Lin's knees.

"Driver, ram that center gap!" Meridian's commander screamed from the shoulder, waving the lead twenty-ton National Guard armored carrier forward past the stalled troopers.

Black diesel exhaust erupted vertical as low-range gears slammed in and four-foot tires crept forward over crushed tear-gas tins straight toward the gap where Lin was tearing Jace's collar open on the road.

Elara let go of Silas's coat and walked six steps straight out in front of Lin's medic cart onto the greasy crown of County Road Nine, planting both muddy boots square in front of the steel plow blade.

Ten feet back behind two-inch bullet-resistant windshield glass sat a nineteen-year-old National Guard corporal with pimples along his jaw beneath his Kevlar rim. Looking down over his hood, the boy could see Trooper Miller standing bare-handed refusing orders on his left, and on his right six gray corporate contractors still swinging steel clubs over a motionless man bleeding out on the centerline while Lin pumped two-handed chest compressions in the mud.

"Push the plow through, Corporal!" Meridian's liaison screamed into the cab intercom loud enough to bleed out the firing port. "Writ's active!"

The nineteen-year-old stared down at Elara's empty blood-smeared hands, then sideways at the gray mercenary raising a drum launcher toward her chest.

The boy yanked the red parking-air knob out with his left fist, killed the master fuel toggle, kicked his armored side door wide open, and stepped out onto the steel fender racking a round into his service rifle, pointed dead center at the Meridian contractor's chest.

"Drop the launcher!" the kid screamed, voice cracking an octave high across the suddenly quiet diesel line. "Drop that fucking launcher right now!"

Sixty miles west inside the packed marble well of the Joint Federal-State Appellate Bench, Chief Judge Halvorsen had brought her gavel down ninety seconds earlier over the unrolled 1854 treaty parchment and forty-two county engineers' famine affidavits, issuing an immediate emergency writ voiding the Governor's Reclamation Order on sovereign treaty co-management land and stripping state law-enforcement immunity from every unit east of Milepost Twelve.

Inside State Patrol Central Dispatch, Shift Supervisor Carla Vance, whose seventy-year-old mother slept dry in River Landing Four, slapped the red all-band tactical override key down hard.

Every roof horn on twelve carriers and every shoulder mic along two hundred yards of smoke crackled live at once:

\begin{center}
\textbf{ALL STATE PATROL AND GUARD UNITS AT COUNTY ROAD NINE:}\\
\textbf{CENTRAL COMMAND OVERRIDE. CEASE AND DESIST IMMEDIATELY.}\\
\textbf{FEDERAL-STATE APPELLATE BENCH HAS VOIDED RECLAMATION WRIT SEVEN-TWELVE UNDER THE 1854 SOVEREIGN TREATY COMPACT.}\\
\textbf{PRIVATE UTILITY CONTRACTORS ON SITE HOLD ZERO LAWFUL AUTHORITY.}\\
\textbf{DETAIN ANY ARMED NON-STATE PERSONNEL USING FORCE AND WITHDRAW WEST OF MILEPOST TWELVE IMMEDIATELY.}
\end{center}

Echoes slapped once off the wet timber ridge and died.

Trooper Dan Miller didn't drop his vest gently; he unsnapped his retention holster, stepped squarely across the centerline beside the Guard corporal's fender, and leveled his nine-millimeter straight at the nearest gray-suited Meridian squad leader. Along the left lane, thirty county and state patrolmen snapped their visors up, turned ninety degrees right, and advanced in a grim blue-and-black wall onto the stunned sixty-man contractor wedge, forcing steel-core clubs clattering onto the gravel and zip-tying corporate wrists behind backs right in the roadside weeds.

Nobody along the valley ranks cheered. Not one voice.

Ten yards behind the sage brazier, in a cleared circle of wet blacktop smelling sharp of pepper gas and copper, Lin stopped chest compressions, sat back slowly on her soaked knees, and pulled the stethoscope tips out of her ears. Across from her, Tomas closed two fingers gently over Jace's open, unseeing eyes and pulled a clean wool blanket up to the faded blue Monroe ink at his collarbones.

Sitting propped upright by Maya's trembling knees with a splint wrap taped hasty around his bruised ribs, Gabe couldn't seem to hear Tahoma's voice announcing the court writ down the road or the armored carriers grinding backward in reverse gear toward the highway. Gabe just kept his bloody right palm resting flat against Jace's cold, inked left wrist on the asphalt, staring dumbly at a stray curl of red cherry shaving still caught inside the cuff seam of Jace's sheepskin pocket.

Elara knelt slowly on the wet tar beside her son, resting one hand on Gabe's shaking neck while looking down at the quiet, angular face of the man who had stood bare-headed on a sleety bridge three weeks ago to keep their town from tearing itself apart, and who had just paid the bill for all four hundred and eighty of them on cold county tar.

Beside her, supporting his broken left collarbone in his coat flap, Silas stood back up only when the last carrier tail-lights vanished east around the Blackwood turnoff. Looking up toward the sixty-foot galvanized utility pylon at the property corner, where four dead aluminum grid lines still stretched east toward Brenner's siding and Meridian's substations, Silas nodded once to Tarn and Marek.

Nobody gave a speech. Tarn and Marek climbed the concrete pier with two battery partner-saws and insulated long-handle shears.

Abrasive wheels screamed shrill for twenty seconds, throwing white sparks down into the frosted ditch weeds until all four steel-core trunk cables parted one after another with sharp, flat rifle cracks, whipping dead into the mud and staying still.

Down on the quiet highway, two state patrolmen carrying saline-rinsed faces helped Doran limp toward the clinic cart alongside five other stretcher cases with cracked ribs and broken jaws, while four foundry men lifted Jace gently onto a clean cedar plank litter on their shoulders to carry him home up the orchard lane.

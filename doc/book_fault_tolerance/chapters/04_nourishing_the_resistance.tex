\chapter{Nourishing the Resistance}











The heat off the steel cone kiln warped the air across the clearing, turning the Douglas firs behind it into a wavering blur. Silas drove an iron pitchfork into a tangle of green alder slash and heaved it over the rim.

Deep in the cone, the wood baked at over a thousand degrees in starved air while rising wood-gas burned across the top lip in a clean, smokeless crown of flame, reducing the timber underneath to black char. Let too much draft in at the bottom and the whole load burned to useless gray ash; choke it too tight and yellow smoke poured across the hillside.

Twenty feet downslope by the compost windrows, Jace tipped a heavy steel wheelbarrow of wet kitchen scraps and woodchips onto the curing pile. His linen sleeves were rolled past his elbows over faded prison tattoos and old venipuncture scars, a canvas bandanna tied low across his forehead against the heat. "Save some slash for the second burn, Silas," Jace called up, scraping his shovel clean against the barrow lip. "Orchard crew wants another forty barrows of charged mulch before the rain hits Friday."

"Tell the orchard crew to come swing a fork," Silas called back, nudging the lower draft damper half an inch shut with the toe of his boot. He tapped the dark refractory collar with a tine. "These cast vents from the Rust Corridor haven't warped a millimeter. Our mild-steel welds would've buckled an hour ago."

Elara wiped soot from her temple with her sleeve, checking the thermocouple trace on her field slate. "Core's steady at five- eighty. Forty more minutes and we quench."

The refractory vents had come in on the morning freight relay from an old mill town three hundred miles east, swapped against the valley's autumn seed garlic and dried medicinal herbs without an invoice changing hands.

"We'll need a second cone before first frost," Silas said, squinting into the shimmer. "The alder coppice grew back thicker than last year."

"Northern sheet-metal shop already has plate rolled for us," Elara said, logging it on her slate.

Forty minutes later, the timer chimed.

"Shut her down!" Elara called.

Silas kicked the base dampers closed, snuffing the crown of flame, then dragged over the two-inch canvas hose from the hillside gravity tank and cracked the brass valve. Cold creek water hit the glowing bed with an explosive hiss. White steam boiled straight up into the fir boughs, carrying the sharp, alkaline tang of wet charcoal and hot iron.

Halfway through the quench, the air-raid horn on the timber watchtower tore across the hollow, followed an instant later by the red emergency strobe on their wrist slates.

Elara looked down at her arm. "Medical alert. Sector Four, down by the drying cribs."

Silas twisted the hose valve shut, dropped the nozzle in the mud, and grabbed the orange trauma canvas off the shelter post while Jace dropped his shovel and vaulted the low stone wall to join them.

"Who's on the wood stack today?" Jace asked as the three of them ran hard down the needle-slick trail through the firs.

"David," Elara panted, reading the telemetry updates flashing across her wrist. "The mechanic who came in Tuesday. Hard-fall trigger and manual panic press. Somebody wrote \textit{log slip, heavy arterial}."

They burst out of the timber into the drying yard. At the foot of the highest Douglas fir crib, two teenage apprentices were straining on cant hooks to keep a green, three-hundred-pound alder log from rolling back onto the man pinned against the concrete pier beneath it.

Jace didn't break stride. He threw his lean weight onto the tail of the wet log beside the boys, muscles cording in his tattooed forearms as he levered the trunk clear off the pier and kicked a wooden wedge underneath to lock it.

David lay in a widening dark stain of sawdust, chalk-white, his right trouser leg split open above the knee where the broken femur pressed against torn muscle. Bright red blood pulsed out with every heartbeat.

Silas dropped onto both knees in the red woodchips and drove his overlapped palms straight into David's right groin crease, leaning all his upper-body weight onto the femoral artery against the pelvic rim.

David arched off the dirt with a ragged cry.

"Jace, hold his shoulders down!" Silas snapped. "Elara, windlass. High as it goes."

Jace knelt behind David's head, gripping the mechanic's shaking shoulders firmly while talking low and steady near his ear: "Stay right here, brother. Eyes on the trees. Don't fight him."

Elara ripped open the trauma pack, slid the black webbing under the top of David's thigh, pulled the slack tight, and handed Silas the aluminum rod. Silas twisted it once, twice, three times, locking the windlass into the catch as David's scream thinned into a dry rattle. Below the strap, the bright spurting stopped, slowing to a dark seep.

"He's crashing," Silas said, checking the weak radial pulse at David's wrist. "Check contraindications. I need volume expander and coagulant in him now."

Elara held her slate two inches from David's wristband, querying the encrypted medical intake record he had entered the day before against \textit{saline dextran, yarrow hemostatic, concentrated salicin}. Three green indicators cleared the screen.

"Clear across the board," Elara said. She pulled a liter IV bag and two wax-sealed amber vials from the padded inner sleeve, noticing the tiny mountain-peak stamp of the Timberline Apothecary high in the Cascades pressed into the foil caps as she thumbed them open.

Silas slid an eighteen-gauge cannula into David's left antecubital vein and taped it down with red fingers. Elara spiked the bag, hung it from Jace's outstretched hand, and pushed the vials into the port.

Over the next ninety seconds, David's violent shivering eased a fraction. His pupils tracked up toward Jace's face.

"Ninety-four over sixty and climbing," Silas said, letting out a breath. "Boys, grab the wheeled litter out of the saw shed. Tell Lin at the clinic to prep the trauma table and call the orthopedic surgeon up from the river hub."

\bigskip

By dusk, the clinic lamps had been turned down low. Lin had irrigated the compound fracture and pinned it in an external traction splint until the river enclave's bone surgeon arrived, and Sarah sat pulled right up against the cot with her forehead resting on David's good arm.

Outside under the eaves, Silas, Elara, and Jace scrubbed dried blood and kiln ash off their arms at the deep stainless wash trough. Jace dried his hands on his shirt hem, gave Elara a brief nod, and headed back down the hill to bank the neglected charcoal cone before dark.

Silas shut off the brass tap. Now that the work was over, a fine tremor ran through his fingers on the rim of the sink.

Elara leaned against the timber wall beside him, watching the pink rinse water swirl down the drain into the gravel sump. Down on the steel tray lay the two empty amber vials from the mountain stills, alongside a forged hose clamp from the eastern Rust Corridor.

"Close thing," Silas said quietly.

"Too close," Elara said, looking out past the orchard lights toward the dark county road beyond the ridge. "Make sure the wood crew re-wedges every crib in Sector Four before morning. Next time we might not be five minutes up the hill."

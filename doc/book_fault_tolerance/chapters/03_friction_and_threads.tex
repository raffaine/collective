\chapter{Friction and Threads}












The walking-foot sewing machine hammered a steady beat through the oak floorboards of the Textile Commons. The loft smelled of warm machine oil, damp wool, and the sharp mineral tang of plant-dye vats simmering in the corner.

Silas sat bent over a wide wooden cutting table near the center of the room, working a heavy steel needle through the stiff collar of a canvas jacket. With each pull, he drew white hemp thread across the indigo fabric in tight Sashiko stitches, reinforcing the shoulder seams where logging chains chewed through the weave.

Elara pushed through the double doors, still chilled from four hours in the basement server room. Her satchel strap cut into her collarbone.

Silas glanced up at the sound of the latch and set his needle down on the waxed paper.

"You look like you've been arguing with the ghost of a city planner all morning," Silas said over the clatter of the looms.

Elara crossed the floor toward the warmth of the steam irons. "Close. County property-tax portal changed its schema overnight without notice. If I hadn't caught the rejection code before noon, our payment would have bounced into an automatic lien."

Silas traced the raised hemp stitching with his thumb. "Who's next on their list?"

"Anyone still solvent," Elara said, looking down at the jacket. "Marcus's coat?"

"Tore the shoulder hauling fence posts yesterday. Cheaper in wool and hours to double-stitch the yoke than weave three yards of new canvas."

"How did Sarah's industrial serger check out?" Elara asked.

"Runs cleaner than ours," Riya called over from the sorting bins.

Riya walked up wiping oil from her palms onto a linen rag, her dark hair pinned up with a wooden bodkin. She and Silas had come up together on the salvage crews during the settlement's third year. She squinted critically at Silas's collar work. "Your tension's pulling tight on the left seam, Silas. We timed the serger before breakfast, Elara. Sarah's sleeping off the road now, but she spent an hour showing the apprentices how to keep the differential feed from puckering heavy hemp. Cotton trimmings went out to the worm bins, and we bagged the nylon scraps for printer stock."

"Good," Elara said.

Riya tapped the table edge. "Let me check the mordant pot before the fleece goes brittle."

Once Riya turned back to the vats, Silas studied Elara's face. "Get away from the terminals for an hour. Walk over to the garage with me while I print a mount for the west gate camera."

Elara glanced instinctively toward her satchel, then let out a breath and nodded. "Half an hour."

The Fabrication Commons occupied an old tractor shed across the lane, smelling of cutting fluid and hot thermoplastics. Along the north wall, enclosed 3D printers hummed behind glass doors alongside a CNC mill shaving aluminum bar stock under a milky stream of coolant.

Silas wiped an open print bed with isopropyl alcohol and held his wrist slate to the frame reader. The display loaded a bracket schematic drafted two winters back by a machinist up in the northern timber settlements.

"Forty-minute print," Silas said as the steppers whined and the brass nozzle laid down its first skirt of melted nylon. He leaned against the bench beside her. "Put half the county filings on Marta and Tobin next quarter, Elara. Nobody's sharp after six days straight in the cellar."

"Marta and Tobin aren't listed as corporate officers on the state charter," Elara said, watching the print head shuttle back and forth. "If the zoning shield cracks, the sheriff serves the person on the incorporation deed."

"Still doesn't mean you do all the night watches alone."

Before she could answer, the slate on her wrist buzzed twice and lit red.

Silas straightened up. "Gate?"

Elara pulled up the alert log. "Code enforcement again. Not housing records this time. They're querying the utility meter bus directly: transformer load, line harmonics, and thermal draw."

Silas swore softly. "Looking for machine tools."

"If they log three-phase motor draw from that mill on an agricultural parcel, they issue an immediate cease-and-desist with daily fines," Elara said, already kneeling beside the wall patch panel and pulling a rugged tablet and patch cord from her bag. "Kill the mill and drop the bay doors. If the meter numbers don't match what they expect, they'll fly an infrared drone over the ridge to check roof heat."

Silas crossed the shop in four strides and slapped the red mushroom switch on the CNC mill. As the spindle whined down, he hauled on the chain hoist until the heavy steel roll-up door clanged shut against the concrete sill, leaving the room lit only by amber emergency strips.

Without the exhaust fans, the trapped heat from the mill and printer casings pressed in fast. Elara jacked hardline Ethernet into the wall port and routed the incoming county meter poll into a pre-recorded telemetry loop mimicking halide seedling lamps and an orchard drip pump.

For twenty seconds neither of them spoke. Sweat ran down Elara's ribs under her shirt while Silas stood by the side door, ear cocked toward the corrugated tin roof for the high buzz of quadcopter rotors.

On the tablet, the polling indicator stalled, polled a second checksum, and turned gray.

\textit{Audit Complete. Status: Agricultural Compliance Verified.}

Elara rested the back of her head against the cinderblock wall while the little printer beside them kept laying down nylon thread.

Silas walked back through the hot dimness, shirt stuck to his back. "Clean?"

"For this afternoon," Elara said.

Silas held out a hand and pulled her up from the concrete.

"Twice in twenty-four hours," he said, looking at the dark screen.

"Municipal bonds defaulted in two neighboring counties last month," Elara said, coiling her patch cord. "They're hunting anything that still has copper in the walls and grain in the bins. Paperwork won't buy us another five years, Silas. Sooner or later we either fight them on the road or cut the trunk lines altogether."

Silas rested one palm on the iron latch of the bay door, waiting another minute before cracking it to the cool air outside.

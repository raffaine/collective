\chapter{New Growth}

Three weeks into March, the frost finally let go of the upper terraces, though nobody in the kitchen was singing much at breakfast yet.

Up behind the potting sheds, Gabe drove the four steel tines of his pitchfork deep into Windrow Two and leaned his hips back on the ash shaft. Crusty brown straw and coffee chaff split open with a wet sigh, rolling a thick white cloud of hundred-and-forty-degree steam straight into the cold morning air. Gabe wiped damp mist off his forehead with his coat sleeve, leaving a smear of black compost across his temple. At seventeen he had shot up another inch since winter solstice, wrists hanging bare past his mended flannel cuffs.

Across the steaming trench, Jace slid his own fork cleanly under a matted slab of goat bedding and flipped it onto the center peak without lifting it higher than his knees.

Dark purple hollows sat bruised deep beneath Jace's eyes. He had come straight up to the compost yards from the four-to-eight watch outside Vane's woodlot cabin, drinking cold chicory out of a mason jar while standing beside the burn barrel to warm stiff knuckles. Even with sleeves rolled up to his elbows in the steam, faded blue cellblock ink running right across old white needle tracks along both forearms, his shoulders looked looser out here in the dirt than anywhere near the hall.

"Lifting too high again, kid," Jace said quietly over the hiss of the opened pile. "Roll it off your thigh. Keep hoisting wet straw with your lower back and you'll walk bent like Corin before your kid learns to crawl."

Gabe blew out a breath and propped his chin on the end of his fork handle. "Haven't thought straight since four-thirty. Midwives moved Maya into Room Four before sunrise. Lin told me to go turn two windrows instead of pacing holes in the clinic floorboards because first babies take all day."

A small, genuine crease broke the tired lines around Jace's mouth. Leaning his fork upright into the warm mulch, he unscrewed his mason jar and offered Gabe the last swallow of cold coffee. "Smart woman, Lin. Pacing wears boots out faster than gravel."

Below their terrace, pale March sun glinted off wet polycarbonate panels being unbolted from the winter greenhouse frames. Down by the orchard wall, Aris sat wrapped in two grey quilts on a cedar bench beside Doran, staring intensely at his right lap as he tried over and over to squeeze a soft ball of wet river clay with fingers that barely curled half an inch while Lin held picture cards up on his knee. Fifty yards farther east, two men from Lodge Four walked past carrying kiln rakes with their eyes fixed straight ahead on the mud ruts, giving the clinic bench a wide berth. Nobody shouted anymore, yet the valley still walked around that corner like a bruised rib.

"Weird bringing a kid into it right now," Gabe admitted quietly, watching the two kiln stokers disappear behind the sheds without looking back. "Two weeks ago I watched Doran nearly swing a tire iron into my mom's ribs on the lower bridge, and county drones flew three low passes over the creek yesterday afternoon."

Jace followed his gaze down toward Aris's quilt on the bench, rubbing his thumb slowly across the pale Monroe scar on his left wrist.

"Beats every place I ever saw outside that ridge, Gabe," Jace said simply.

Gabe glanced back across the steam. Everyone knew Jace had walked over the western pass three autumns ago half-dead off highway fentanyl and ten years of county lockups, though Jace rarely talked about it unless asked.

"Back in the metro," Jace said, kicking a dry clump of alder bark into the hot center of the pile, "court rehabs were just holding tanks with inspirational posters taped over barred windows. Piss dirty once after a nightmare, or miss a Tuesday fee to a private probation clerk, and they shipped you straight back to Monroe in leg irons. Either you played cured saint on their clipboard or you were garbage."

He drove his fork back into the windrow, voice staying flat and conversational. "First three nights I sweated withdrawal out in Room One down there, I kept waiting for Silas to bolt the shutters from the outside. Instead Lin left the latch open, brewed strong cannabis-stem tea so I could keep broth down, and old Olander sat in a chair whittling cedar pegs till sunrise so I wouldn't climb the walls alone. Six months later, when the shakes were gone and the uglier stuff underneath started coming up, Miriam sat twelve hours beside me on blankets with measured psilocybin tea from the drying loft while I cried forty years of poison out."

Jace paused, looking straight across the rising steam at Gabe without a trace of softness. "Didn't turn me into an angel, kid. Couple nights last week sitting in sleet outside Vane's door listening to him mutter through the planks, that old itch scratched plenty loud behind my ears. Difference here isn't nobody gets ugly or wants a drink after supper. Difference is when somebody breaks a bone, or breaks their own head, nobody makes fifty bucks a day locking 'em in a concrete box and walking away. We're stuck looking at each other in the morning."

Before Gabe could answer, the worn slate strapped to his left wrist gave two bright, sharp chimes.

Gabe yanked his sleeve back so fast he nearly dropped his glove into the mulch:

\begin{center}
\textbf{CLINIC ROOM FOUR, LIN:}\\
\textbf{SHE'S CLOSE. WASH YOUR HANDS AND GET DOWN HERE.}
\end{center}

For one full second Gabe stood frozen with his mouth open, staring at the two lines of black text.

Jace let out a short, barking laugh, reached across the trench, and yanked the pitchfork clean out of Gabe's numb fingers. "Move your boots, Papa! Pile'll still be hot tomorrow!"

Gabe bolted straight down the grass bank without taking the steps.

Vaulting the low dry-stone wall below the berry canes, he tore down the muddy wheel lane past the sawmill where two carpenters raised planing irons in salute and whistled shrill through their teeth after him, dodged sideways around Bryn's slow-rolling battery flatbed stacked with kiln brick, and hit the swept gravel path through the winter rosemary beds at a dead run.

Outside the clinic porch, Aris looked up from his quilt as Gabe thundered up the wooden steps; Aris lifted his good left hand two inches off his knee and tapped his chest once in quiet luck as Gabe pushed through the heavy cedar doors.

Inside the entry hall, boiled linen and woodsmoke warmed the air. Elara stepped out of the laundry room carrying a stack of warm cotton squares fresh off the stove rack, foundry grease scrubbed pink off her knuckles, her tired face breaking instantly into an unguarded grin at the compost stuck in her son's hair.

"Scrub to the elbows at the copper trough first," Elara ordered, pointing her chin toward the soap pump. "Silas is boiling water down the hall. Go on."

Forty seconds later, arms still dripping clean well water, Gabe eased open the latch to Room Four.

Afternoon sun slanted through high south glass across curved earthen walls. No steel stirrups or buzzing ceiling tubes cluttered the floor: only a low wide pine bedstead, stacked warm towels, and a pocket Doppler speaker ticking a fast, steady little \textit{lub-dub, lub-dub} beside a basin of hot sage water.

Maya knelt forward on the mattress gripping a braided linen pull-strap looped over the ceiling beam, dark curls plastered wet to her neck and cheeks flushed deep red.

"Took you... ten minutes," Maya panted between contractions, turning her head far enough to catch his wet hand as Gabe dropped onto both knees beside the bedframe. "You smell like... goat straw."

"Scrubbed the arms, forgot the shirt," Gabe said, laughing raggedly as Maya's fingers clamped down on his wrist hard enough to dent skin when the next wave rolled down her back.

At the foot of the mattress, Lin checked position with warm, unhurried hands, speaking low and steady. "Right on the crown now, Maya. Chin down when this wave builds, push slow through the bottom of it. Nobody's rushing you."

Gabe pressed his damp forehead against Maya's trembling upper arm while she tucked her chin hard to her collarbones and bore down with a long, gravelly groan deep from her diaphragm that lasted ten full seconds until the pine rope creaked overhead.

"Head's clear, wait one breath for the shoulder," Lin murmured, hands turning once with practiced certainty. "Little push now. There!"

Maya sagged back onto the bolsters gasping for air, eyes wide on the foot of the bed.

Half a second of absolute quiet hung in the sunlit room, followed by an outraged, sputtering squall as wet newborn lungs hit cool March air and bellowed full volume.

Lin didn't cut the cord right away or carry her to a cold tray; wiping nose and mouth once with warm flannel, she lifted the slick, dark-haired little body straight up onto Maya's bare sternum and pulled two heated cotton blankets snug over both of them.

"Oh," Maya whispered, voice breaking completely as both shaking hands came up over the tiny wet back. "Oh, hi. Hi, Clara. Look at your hair."

Under the warm cloth, the furious crying hitched twice, hiccuped into a tiny squeak, and quieted against the thud of Maya's ribs.

Gabe leaned ten inches closer over the mattress rail, throat locked so tight he couldn't have said his own name for gold. Clara's crumpled red fist poked out from the edge of the heated towel, fingers smaller than pine needles curling and uncurling blindly in the sunbeam until Gabe touched his scrubbed index knuckle lightly to her palm and all five tiny fingers latched reflexively shut around his skin.

Behind him, the cedar door clicked open two inches. Silas stood bare-headed in the corridor with coal-dust scrubbed pink off his forehead, resting one heavy palm silently on Gabe's shoulder while Elara stood right beside him looking down at her granddaughter with wet, shining eyes she didn't bother wiping.

Just after sundown, once Maya had fallen deep asleep on clean sheets and Clara lay swaddled tight inside a woven willow basket beside Gabe's low wooden rocker, a soft tap sounded at the unlatched door.

Jace stood in the dim corridor lamplight in his heavy night-watch sheepskin coat, hair combed flat with cold water and hands scrubbed raw pink up to the tattooed wrists. Balanced gingerly on his open palm sat a hand-carved wooden bird no bigger than a sparrow, pared out of seasoned red cherry scrap and sanded with horsetail rush until the wings gleamed dull satin on a balanced hemp thread.

Looking at sleeping Maya, Jace didn't say a word. Stepping lightly across the stone flags in muddy boots he paused to wipe twice on the threshold mat, he hung the hemp loop from the bent willow hood of the bassinet, looked down for five quiet seconds at Clara's sleeping face, and tapped two knuckles once against his own breastbone toward Gabe before slipping back out into the cold hall toward his midnight shift at the north woodlot.

Gabe reached out with one finger and touched the cherry tailfeather.

Balanced to the grain, the little wooden bird dipped forward, lifted its wings without a sound in the stove draft, and kept rocking steadily above the basket long after Jace's footsteps faded down the gravel lane.

For Gabe, a ``school day'' didn't involve a ringing bell or a row of cramped desks. It began in the Learning Grove—a wide, open-air amphitheater built into the roots of the Iron Canopy, where the air was scented with damp earth and the quiet hum of localized data-servers. In the Port, the Body of Knowledge (BoK) was an immersive experience. Education wasn't a preparation for a job; it was the process of becoming a more effective node in the mesh.

Today's domain was \textit{Systemic Defense}, but not the kind involving walls or weapons. Gabe sat in a circle with his peer-group, their translucent tablets reflecting the dappled sunlight. They were analyzing the ``Immune Response'' of a neighboring cell that had recently dealt with a localized logic-leak. There was no ``teacher'' at the front of the room asserting authority. Instead, a Mentor—a veteran system architect who had spent decades in the Seattle underground—facilitated the discussion, ensuring that every child's query was indexed and addressed by the BoK's recursive logs.

% --- Run 2: Expert Coexistence ---

What Gabe loved most about the Grove was the lack of barriers. As his group debated the ethics of ``Automated Rerouting,'' he noticed a cluster of adults a few yards away, deeply involved in a high-level audit of the Tidal Gateway's sensor mesh. In the Legacy System, children were segregated from the ``real work'' of adults. Here, the BoK nodes encouraged overlap. Gabe could listen in on the experts' stream, and if he had a question that his peer-group couldn't resolve, he was encouraged to approach the adults. 

He watched an elder technician explain a complex Fourier transform to a ten-year-old as if they were peers. In the Collective, expertise wasn't a gate to be guarded; it was a resource to be shared. The democratic nature of the node meant that the ``Truth'' wasn't dictated by the person with the highest Value standing, but by the person whose data-model held the most structural integrity. Gabe felt a surge of pride as he contributed a localized observation about the vertical orchards' water-pressure to the adults' discussion. They didn't patronize him; they integrated his data point into the live model. In the Grove, every perspective was a potential patch for a future bug.

% --- Run 3: The Arrival ---

The peaceful rhythm of the morning was broken by a shift in the ``Social Resonance.'' Gabe felt it before he saw it—a ripple of tension moving through the adults, like a cold wind through the leaves. Three figures were approaching from the Logistics Hub. Elara led them, but her usual fluid grace had been replaced by a sharp, defensive armor. Behind her walked two men who looked like they belonged to a different timeline.

One was a young Asian man, perhaps in his early thirties, with a face that seemed carved from cold stone. He wasn't particularly tall, but Jax moved with a mechanical precision that refused to acknowledge the beauty of the Grove. He didn't have the scars of a brawler; his ``War Mode'' was in his eyes—restless, scanning, and entirely devoid of the Port's relaxed resonance. Beside him was Kael, an older man whose silver hair hung loose to his shoulders. He didn't wear the heavy, rain-slicked gear of the Seattle underground; instead, he wore a rugged, multi-pocketed leather vest over a simple shirt. He looked like someone familiar with the nature of the Port, as an old relative coming for a visit.

``The taskforce is here,'' Gabe heard the Mentor whisper, the holographic logs in the center of the circle flickering as a new ``Security Protocol'' was initiated by the Society Node. ``They're linking with the BoK primary. Something's in the water, children. Or rather, something's in the code.''

% --- Run 4: The Investigation Begins ---

Elara spotted Gabe and made a brief, tight-lipped gesture for him to stay back, but curiosity was a fundamental requirement of a BoK student. Gabe watched as Jax and Kael set up their rig in the center of the Grove, right next to the children's meditation mats. They weren't there to teach; they were there to hunt. 

\textit{They look like ghosts,} Gabe thought, watching Jax's fingers move over a screen that was bleeding a strange, oily black data-stream. \textit{They look like they brought the Seattle rain with them.} 

% --- Chapter 07: The Ghost Signature (Run 04: The Technical Friction) ---

Jax looked up, his gaze meeting Gabe's for a second. There was no ``Peace Mode'' in those eyes. ``We found an anomaly,'' Jax said, his voice grating against the quiet hum of the Grove. ``A signature that doesn't belong to the mesh. It's a `Ghost'—a recursive call that's been pinging our sensors since the pump-failure.'' He looked back at Elara, his expression grim. ``It's not a glitch, Elara. It's a systemic bleed. Something in the extended Seattle area is pulling Value out of the neighboring cells, and it's using our gateway's resonance to mask the transit.''

Jax's fingers blurred across the terminal, isolating a jagged, stuttering frequency. ``It's not looking for a person. It's looking for a leak. It's been harvesting the metabolic surplus we've been sending to the interior. If we don't trace this back to the source, the Port isn't just a hub—it's an unwitting battery for a Legacy revival.''

% --- Run 05: The Veteran’s Touch ---

Kael stepped forward then, his silver hair catching the light as he moved with the easy, grounded grace of someone who had walked these boardwalks many times before. He didn't stand guard like Jax; he sat down on the edge of the meditation mat, bringing himself to eye-level with Gabe and the other students. He didn't look like a hunter. He looked like an elder returning to a conversation he had briefly stepped away from.

``It's a puzzle, isn't it?'' Kael said softly, his voice a calm anchor in the rising tension. He gestured to the oily blue data-stream Jax had isolated. ``In the Body of Knowledge, we learn that energy always leaves a footprint. What Jax is showing you isn't a ghost to be feared. It's a `Shadow Task'—a set of instructions that has forgotten who it belongs to. We need your help to understand the local terrain of these logic-paths.''

He looked at the Mentor and the students, including them in the gravity of the moment without stripping away their sovereignty. ``We aren't here to hide the data from you. We're here to audit it together. You know the metabolic rhythm of this Port better than any outsider. This is probably not a recent development, so more than identifying what has caused it, we need to understand it.''

% --- Chapter 07: The Ghost Signature (Run 06: Collaborative Audit - Revised) ---

Gabe felt the knot in his stomach loosen. Kael wasn't treating them like liabilities; he was treating them like the primary sensors they were. The Grove transformed from a schoolroom into a war-room, but the atmosphere remained democratic. Elara joined them, sitting beside Kael, her posture finally losing some of its rigid ``War Mode'' as she saw the familiar way Kael worked with the community.

``Kael is right,'' the Mentor added, adjusting the BoK's visualization. ``We've been looking for a break in the system, but the system isn't broken. It's functioning perfectly—it's just performing a Task we didn't authorize or which demand hasn't been sanctioned.”

Gabe looked at the flowing data-streams, trying to see them the way Kael did—not as errors, but as a phenomenon. ``I was looking at the vertical orchards' growth cycles,'' Gabe volunteered, pulling his personal project logs onto the central display. ``Over the last three months, the nutrient pumps have been running at a 2\% higher metabolic rate than the harvest justifies. I thought it was just seasonal friction, a calibration error in the soil sensors. But if you map it against the outgoing logistics to the Seattle-fringe cells...''

Kael leaned in, his silver hair brushing Gabe's shoulder as he examined the overlay. ``The surplus isn't being lost,'' Kael murmured, his eyes bright with discovery. ``It's being `mirrored.' Every time the Port sends a legitimate shipment, a ghost-request is generated in the shadow of the transaction. It's a rhythmic bleed, so subtle that the Service Nodes treat it as background noise.''

% --- Run 07: Conclusion — The Dilemma Forms ---

The audit continued deep into the afternoon. By the time the sun began to set, the BoK node had mapped the phenomenon to a cluster of dead-zones on the edge of the old Seattle grid. It wasn't a sudden attack; it was a parasitic integration. A group of Legacy remnants—or perhaps a rogue algorithmic node—had successfully mapped the Port's generosity and turned it into an extraction vector.

Kael stood up, his hand resting briefly on Gabe's shoulder. ``You've done well today. You've helped us find the `Where.' Now we have to decide the `How.''' 

Jax looked at the map, his face tight. ``We can try to patch it from here, but as Kael said, this isn't a bug. It's a design flaw in our transparency that they're exploiting. If we stay, we're just playing defense while they perfect the siphon. If we go, we're taking `War Mode' back to a place that doesn't have a metabolic floor to catch us if we fall.”

Gabe looked at Elara. He saw the flicker of the old Seattle rain in her eyes—the weight of the choice. For the first time, the Port felt small. The resilience they had built was beautiful, but as the jagged blue line of the shadow-task continued to pulse on the screen, Gabe realized that peace wasn't just something you maintained. Sometimes, it was something you had to go out and fight for.

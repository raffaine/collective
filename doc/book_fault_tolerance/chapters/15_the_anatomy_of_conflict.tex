\chapter{The Anatomy of Conflict}

Six weeks into the winter power ration, tempers across the lower yards had worn thin as scrap tin.

Every cabin heat loop still dropped to fifty-two degrees after nine at night. Because Auxiliary Wheel Two couldn't pull both the pottery kilns and the biochar retorts during daylight clinic hours, the lower firing crews had spent all of February working split midnight-to-six turnarounds in drafty thirty-eight-degree sheds while sleet rattled the tin roofs.

Late that Tuesday afternoon, carrying a crate of sprouted seed potatoes down the muddy terrace path with Silas thirty yards below her by the culvert ditch, Elara heard the shouting start behind Retort Shed Three. Before she could set the crate down, a single wet, heavy crack cut the voices off short.

Steel on bone.

Elara dropped the wooden crate into the slush, seed tubers bouncing into the drainage rut, and ran flat-out around the stacked alder cordwood.

Silas vaulted the low stone retaining wall from the lower track three strides ahead of her.

In the trampled mud and black charcoal grit between the kiln firebox and the clay-settling tanks, twenty-six-year-old Aris lay curled on his right side, convulsing violently. Bright red blood pumped between his clay-dusted fingers where he clutched the left side of his jaw and temple, pooling dark across the frozen woodchips.

Standing over him with his chest heaving under a soot-stiffened canvas vest was Vane, one of the senior retort stokers. Dangling from Vane's right fist was a two-foot forged iron damper wrench, wet red hair and blood clinging to its square jaw.

"Drop the iron!" Silas barked, not breaking stride as he drove his good right shoulder straight into Vane's ribs, knocking the stoker flat onto his back in the slush and sending the heavy wrench skittering into the gravel.

Vane didn't cry out; he rolled onto one elbow gasping for wind, soot-streaked face twisted tight and white around the mouth. "Took his hand to my main damper!" Vane spat wildly up at Silas. "Sixteen hours I been nursing this firing and he shuts my draft off cold!"

Elara didn't look at Vane. Dropping onto both knees beside Aris in the red mud, she saw instantly it wasn't just a broken jaw. Above the shattered left mandible, four inches of skin over Aris's left temple and ear had split open over a sunken, spongy hollow in the skull bone. Every ragged breath rattled wetly at the back of his throat, and his right boot scraped spasmodically once in the gravel before going limp.

"Medic!" Elara yelled up toward the greenhouse ridge, easing Aris's head three inches forward with both palms so blood and broken teeth drained onto the woodchips instead of down his windpipe without pressing on the depressed bone over his ear. "Trauma team to Retort Three! Head strike!"

Thirty seconds later, boots crashed down through the cold frames. Lin and Tomas vaulted the terrace wall with orange canvas jump-bags banging against their hips, mud still caked on their greenhouse aprons.

"Slide back, Elara, give me his head," Lin snapped, dropping onto her knees and tearing a suction bulb and curved airway tube out of the top pouch.

Elara backed off onto her heels, staring stupidly for a second at Aris's dark blood cooling across both her wrists.

Lin and Tomas didn't waste half a word.

"Left temporal-parietal depression, pupils unequal. Left's blowing."

"Jaw's comminuted. Suctioning."

"Keep C-spine neutral, pack around the bone ring, zero direct pressure over the dent."

When pain dragged a terrible, garbled moan out of Aris's chest and his left arm flailed upward toward his face, Silas left Vane sitting in the mud under two arriving woodcutters' eyes and knelt across Aris's left shoulder, pinning the potter's good wrist gently against the dirt while Tomas slid a sixteen-gauge needle into his forearm.

"Intracranial pressure's climbing fast," Lin said tightly as an electric cargo trike rigged with a litter frame skidded around the cordwood pile. "If we don't take a burr-hole drill to that epidural bleed inside twenty minutes, he herniates on the hill. Load on three: one, two, lift!"

Four pairs of hands lifted Aris flat onto the backboard. Lin swung onto the moving flatbed straddling his hips to bag air manually into his lungs as the trike whined full-throttle up the steep gravel grade toward the clinic surgical room.

Dead quiet dropped back over the smoking kilns, broken only by steam hissing softly from a cracked mortar joint on Retort Three.

Down in the black slush lay two white molars and Aris's woolen cap.

Vane sat propped on one palm beside the cordwood pile, wiping a smear of his own split-lip blood off his chin. Two other night-shift kiln hands, Marek and Cole, stood three paces behind him by the firebox door with long stoking poles still in their mittens, faces gray and hollow from three weeks of four-hour sleeps.

On Elara's forearm, her slate buzzed three sharp, flat pulses. Around the yard, Marek's, Cole's, and Silas's slates buzzed in unison.

Once Lin logged life-threatening head trauma at the clinic door and two eyewitnesses at the drying racks tapped confirmation, the settlement's automatic safety lockout didn't wait for a council meeting:

\textbf{SAFETY BOUNDARY ACTIVE: SEVERE BODILY HARM}\\
\textbf{SUBJECT: VANE (KILN BAY THREE)}\\
\textbf{ALL WORKSHOP, TOOLSHED, VEHICLE \& COMMON KEYS REVOKED}\\
\textbf{CABIN HEAT \& KITCHEN RATION KEYS UNCHANGED}
\end{center}

Every solenoid latch on the Foundry, the machine shop, the saw sheds, and the tractor pool had just gone dead to Vane's wrist fob. Nobody owned handcuffs in the valley, and nobody could turn off a man's food ration or freeze his bunk, yet within ten seconds Vane could no longer open a single door holding heavy iron.

"Stand up, Vane," Silas said, standing over him with Aris's blood smeared dark across the knee of his canvas trousers.

Vane got to his feet on his own before Silas could touch his collar, shrugging angrily back half a step when one of the woodcutters reached near his elbow.

"Don't paw at me," Vane snarled, voice hoarse from woodsmoke. "Kid came marching down from the pottery wheel waving an amp-allocation slip and kicked my primary air slide shut mid-burn! Ruined two tons of oak char I stood freezing over since two this morning!"

"He throws clay filter candles on a kick-wheel because we shut power off to the pottery bay three weeks ago, Vane," Elara said, her voice dangerously flat. "And you caved the side of his skull in with four pounds of forged steel."

"He grabbed my wrist when I reached to open the slide back up!" Vane shouted across the yard, looking sideways toward Marek and Cole for backing. "Ask Marek! Kid's been lording water and kiln amps over the lower sheds all month while uphill clerks sit warm in basement server rooms!"

Marek looked down at the bloody woodchips, shifted his boots uncomfortably, and didn't say yes or no.

"Walk," Silas said quietly, pointing up the lane toward the textile mill. "Elders are already ringing the hall."

Word had outrun them up the hill. Along the weaving porch and outside the wash troughs, thirty people stood motionless in the grey February dusk watching Vane walk between Silas and Elara. Nobody yelled curses, though young Sam from the electronics bay stared at the dried blood on Elara's cuffs with his mouth slightly open as if watching a bridge cable snap in slow motion.

Inside the main depot hall, nobody had lit the big masonry stoves yet; the vast cedar room sat at forty-six degrees, smelling of cold ash and wool. Under a single hanging battery lamp in the middle of the sawdust floor sat seven rough wooden chairs. Miriam, Corin, Marta, and Elias were already seated, while forty shaken neighbors stood three deep along the north wall.

Vane dropped heavily onto the empty chair facing Miriam, crossing both soot-blackened arms tight over his ribs instead of looking at the floor.

"Tomas just ran word across from the clinic," Miriam said into the cold room. Her voice didn't tremble, though both hands gripped the top of her ash stick hard. "Lin drilled two relief holes above Aris's left ear and pulled a clot the size of a plum off his brain. His heart is beating on its own, and they wired his left jaw shut. Whether he wakes up before tomorrow, or what that clot tore inside his head before they reached it, nobody knows tonight."

Along the north wall, Lia pressed a mitten over her mouth; beside her, Aris's bench partner Doran went chalk-white, leaning both shoulders hard against a locust post.

Corin looked straight across six feet of scuffed pine at Vane. "Speak for yourself, Vane. Plain truth, no crooked corners."

Vane stared back at the old man with bloodshot, sleepless eyes. If anyone in the room expected tears or begged forgiveness, they didn't know what six weeks of freezing boots and bruised pride looked like inside a stubborn skull.

"Already told 'em down at the shed," Vane said loudly, chin jutting out. "Been pulling double turnarounds in thirty-eight-degree sleet since Turbine One blew up in January while half this town sleeps till seven. Had Retort Three right at exothermic peak after fourteen hours on my feet, and Aris walks in waving some clinic slip saying my draft fan's pulling twelve amps over quota and kicks my damper shut with his boot. Grabbed my sleeve when I went to pull it back. Yeah, I swung! Shouldn't have had iron in my hand, fine, bad luck where it caught him, except don't sit there in dry wool acting like you didn't push the lower sheds till somebody snapped!"

Silence hung sharp as broken glass across the cold hall.

"Nobody pushed an iron wrench into a twenty-six-year-old boy's temple except your right arm, Vane," Marta said from the farm chair, her weathered face like flint. "Half this valley is working fourteen-hour shifts in cold boots. Tobin lost three fingers off his hand in that river and hasn't raised his voice at an apprentice once."

"We don't own cages here, and we don't hire county sheriffs to haul our messes out of sight," Miriam said, rising slowly onto her stick. "Starting tonight, Vane, your workshop, shed, and hall keys stay dead. You move your bedroll to the isolated perimeter cabin by the north woodlot with two stewards keeping watch outside your door in shifts. Starting at six tomorrow morning, every working hour of your back goes to clearing frozen drainage culverts and splitting cordwood to cover Aris's household and clinic heat. And the morning Aris opens his eyes, you will sit in a chair beside his cot and look straight at whatever your hand left of him."

Vane let out a short, ugly bark through his nose and stood straight up, kicking his wooden chair back six inches across the sawdust.

"Dig ditches under armed babysitters like a chain gang while Harken's quitters lie warm on study-room cots eating our barley?" Vane spat dark pink phlegm onto the planks beside his boot. "Go to hell, old woman. You got no police badge and you got no court paper. Compact says every member keeps roof and rations, and I helped lay the brick on those retorts with my own hands. Try dragging me out of Lodge Four tonight and see how many lower-bay men keep firing your kilns tomorrow!"

"Sit down, Vane," Silas said, taking one step forward, his low voice dropping ten degrees colder than the room.

"Or what, Silas? Gonna take a wrench to my head too so we're even?" Vane sneered, spreading both empty soot-stained palms wide so the forty people along the wall could see. "Go ahead! Show everybody who runs the muscle when the speeches wear thin!"

Elara caught Silas's right wrist with both icy hands before he took a second step. Beneath his canvas cuff, the tendons stood rigid as iron rod. If Silas laid one fist on Vane inside this circle, every rule keeping four hundred desperate people together without police tore straight down the middle.

"Walk him to the north perimeter cabin tonight, Jace and Tarn," Miriam ordered evenly, not flinching at Vane's shout. "If he walks past the west cattle guard onto the county highway, nobody stops him; his name comes off every watershed roll from here to the coast. If he stays inside our fences tonight, he sleeps at the north cabin until a full twelve-person lot jury sits Thursday morning."

Jace stepped quietly out from the end of the wall beside big Tarn from the Foundry. Neither man carried a stick; they simply walked up on either side of Vane and waited.

Vane glared from Miriam to Elara, then looked deliberately past them toward the doorway where Marek, Cole, and four other soot-faced kiln and sawmill workers stood watching with folded arms and unreadable, sullen faces.

"Thursday, then," Vane muttered, wiping his split lip on his forearm before stalking out between Jace and Tarn into the freezing dark without once saying Aris's name.

Nobody in the hall moved for half a minute after the heavy wool curtain swung shut. Along the north wall, Aris's friend Doran pushed off the locust post, stared hard at Elara as if she had just let a rabid dog walk out on a string, and shoved out the side door toward the clinic without a word.

Alone by the unlit stove once the elders dispersed, Silas dipped a clean rag into a cold water bucket, knelt beside Elara's bench, and scrubbed Aris's dried maroon blood off her knuckles until the water ran pink.

"He isn't sorry," Elara said quietly, staring down at the wet rag. "Not by an inch."

Silas wrung the red cloth out into the bucket and looked toward the dark doorway where the kiln hands had stood. "Worse than that," he said. "Half of Lodge Four looked like they agreed with him."

## 1. Problem Statement, Legacy Failure & The Permeable Inclusive Commons

In late-stage extractive economies (Layer 7)—and in flawed historical intentional communities—how people enter and belong to society suffers from four deep failures:
1.  **Surveillance Onboarding & The Cold-Start Debt Trap:** Entering modern economic life requires surrendering biometric identity, credit scores, and background checks to corporate monopolies, while newcomers without capital are locked out of tools and shelter.
2.  **The "Cult Rupture" Fallacy of All-or-Nothing Communes:** Many historical utopian experiments failed because they demanded that newcomers immediately sever all ties with the outside world, quit their jobs, and hand over their life savings. This alienated working parents, caretaking relatives, and gradual adopters who wanted to contribute without abandoning their existing responsibilities.
3.  **Dynastic Feudalism vs. Forced Conscription of the Native-Born:** Communities that survive past the first generation often fall into two traps: either founders' children inherit unearned status (creating an oligarchic dynasty), or youth are pressured into staying without ever making an autonomous adult choice.
4.  **Productivist Ableism:** Both capitalist wage markets and naive labor-hour cooperatives often measure human worth by raw physical output—marginalizing disabled, chronically ill, neurodivergent, deaf/blind, or mobility-impaired citizens as "dependents" rather than equal co-creators of the commons.
